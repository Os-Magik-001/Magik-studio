description: Turning a script into a shoot plan a videographer can follow on set without missing a shot
agents: scriptwriter

**Each scene**
- Where, with whom, and when if the light matters ("before opening, 6:30, window light").
- Framing: wide, medium, close-up or extreme close-up; the angle and height; 9:16 vertical.
- Movement: tripod, handheld, gimbal push, slider. One movement per shot.
- The action in front of the camera, step by step.
- The lines to say there, exactly as in the script.
- Notes: takes to get (at least 2 of every spoken line, 3 of the hook), safety margins (record 2 s before and after), what not to forget.

**Coverage**
- For every beat: one wide, one tight, one detail. The editor needs choices.
- Hook shots first, while energy is high. Group the rest by location to save time.
- Leave room in the frame for text: keep faces and products out of the top 15% and bottom 25% of the 9:16 frame.

**B-roll, sound and props**
- A B-roll list of 6–12 short shots (3–5 s each) that can cover any line: hands, textures, steam, the sign, the street, the team.
- Sound: a lav mic for spoken lines; 10 s of room tone per location; the natural sounds that make the video (the crack, the pour, the bell).
- Props and wardrobe: what to bring and what to avoid (logos of other brands, busy patterns).
