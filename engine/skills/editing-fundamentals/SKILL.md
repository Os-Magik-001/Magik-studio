**Opening**
- Open on one subject doing one complete action: it starts, builds and lands. Don't open on several things competing for attention.

**Choosing shots**
- Every shot must add something new. Drop repeated shots and repeated lines, even good ones.
- Show real actions, like pouring, typing or using the product, at the speed a person actually does them, so viewers can follow along.

**Rhythm**
- Energy comes from contrast, not constant speed:
  - Alternate high-energy and calm shots through the middle of the video.
  - Back-to-back high-energy shots read as noise.
- When you want urgency, make a run of cuts get steadily shorter, for example 1.5s, 1.2s, 0.9s, 0.7s. Evenly spaced cuts feel like a slideshow.
  - Show the same subject from different framings during the run, so viewers always know what they're looking at.
  - Use a run like this once per video, then hold the next shot for at least 1s.

**Holds**
- Plan holds when you set durations, not as leftovers.
  - After a key visual or a title lands, hold it at least 1s before cutting.
  - After a fast burst, rest about 0.5s.
- When unsure, cut one notch slower than your instinct. Editors and clients ask for "hold that longer" far more often than "too slow".

**Arc**
- Default shape:
  1. A clear opening.
  2. One slow, high-quality hero moment.
  3. A middle that alternates energy.
  4. The peak at the end, just before the end card.
- Put the densest information right before the finale.

**Style**
- Use each stylistic device, such as a speed ramp, a fast punch-in or a flashy transition, as the main feature at most once per video. Repeating it makes it a gimmick.
- Keep motion continuous across cuts. If something leaves the frame to the right, the next shot should pick it up moving the same way.
