description: When to punch in, push in or pull out, and where to set the focus point
formats: all
---

**Focus point: set it on every shot**
- The frame is cropped to the target aspect ratio around `focus`. Converting horizontal footage to 9:16 keeps only about a third of the width, so a centered crop can cut off a person standing left of center.
- Look at the contact sheet. Put `focus.x` and `focus.y` on the subject's face, the product or the action. Use 0.5, 0.5 only when the subject really is centered.
- Keep any text that's already in the shot, like signs or screens, fully inside the crop, or crop it out cleanly.

**A slow push says "look at this"**
- `zoom.start` 1.0 to `zoom.end` 1.10–1.25 over a shot of 2s or more.
- Use it on hero shots, emotional beats or a product reveal.
- Pull out (for example 1.25 to 1.0) to reveal context or end a section.

**A static punch-in says "look at THIS"**
- Set the same zoom at start and end (1.2 to 1.4) on the next shot from the same clip. It works like a second camera angle.
- This is the standard way to cut inside one long take, like a talking head or a demo, without a jarring jump cut.
- Alternate between the wide framing (1.0) and the punched-in one (about 1.3) at natural pauses or on emphasis.
- Use it at most twice per 30s for emphasis. In podcast clips you can use it more often to hide cuts.
- Hold the wide framing at least 1s before a punch-in, and the punched-in framing at least 1.5s after.

**Quality limits**
- Stay at or under 1.4 on 1080p footage and 1.6 on 4K. Closer looks soft, especially on faces and text.
- Don't zoom into footage that's already low resolution or heavily compressed.
- Keep frames head-on and steady when they're full of information, like a screen, a menu or a label.
