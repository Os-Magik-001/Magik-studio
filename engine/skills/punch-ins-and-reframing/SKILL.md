**Focus point: set it on every shot**
- The frame is cropped to the target aspect ratio around `focus`. Horizontal footage in a vertical video keeps only about a third of its width, so a centered crop can cut someone off.
- Put `focus` on the face, the product or the action, as seen on the contact sheet.
- Choose the crop for each shot; never assume the center.
- Keep text in the shot fully inside the crop, or crop it out cleanly.
- In vertical video, keep the subject between about 12% and 75% of the frame height. The app's UI covers the bottom.
- When two speakers or a slide must both be seen, flag it in `open_questions`: a 1:1 or 16:9 cut may serve better than 9:16.

**The size change at a cut inside one take**
- Either keep the size (a raw jump cut) or change it clearly. A raw jump at the same `zoom` is normal between two sentences or list items: 9 in 10 of the jump cuts in the studio's top comedy references change size by under 3%. A deliberate second camera changes by at least 0.3 (1.0 to 1.3-1.6, or back); the engine widens a smaller change inside one take to 0.3, because 1.1 or 1.15 reads as a glitch.
- **Never 0.08-0.2 on a face:** it reads as a glitch (the studio's clients sent back cuts at 1.0 / 1.1 / 1.15). The trim pass hides the long pauses it cuts with a 0.14 punch-in; if that jump should be raw or clearly bigger, cut the pause yourself with two segments.
- Better still, cut while the camera or the subject moves (a step, a turn, a pan), and never in the middle of a camera swing that then continues.

**How far: faces and people**
- A **static emphasis punch-in** on a key word, number or claim in a calm interview is 1.08-1.15, rare. Past about 1.18 on a face it starts to feel like a zoom effect. Return to the wider framing when the line ends.
- An **emphasis zoom** in a social reel (the realtor and creator references): about 1.5-1.7 on the key line or number, in 0.3-0.5 s, held 1-2 s, back out in 0.5-0.7 s, at most once per 10-15 s. Build it from three contiguous segments of the same take: `zoom` {1.0, 1.6} over the 0.3-0.5 s that end on the word, {1.6, 1.6} for 1-2 s, {1.6, 1.0} over 0.5-0.7 s, within the clip's sharp-zoom limit.
- A **slow push** over a held shot is 1.00 to 1.05 when calm, and up to 1.10-1.12 for a standard push over 2-3 s. Never more than about 1.15. A pull-out (1.10 to 1.00) reveals context or ends a section.
- A **second camera size** on a talking head is a close framing of about 1.3-1.6, if the clip's sharp-zoom limit allows. Alternate it with the wide framing at phrase changes (see talking-head).
- **Comedy:** in dialogue skits a punchline gets a new shot (the face, the confessional, the listener), not a zoom; a static punch-in of 1.1-1.25 belongs on the reaction hold. In a series filmed in one locked wide, a dialogue shot over 6 s gets a static punch-in of 1.3-1.35 at the next speaker change; once per episode a static snap to 1.6-1.8 on a stunned listener. Group gags and meme skits stay in the full wide: 0 of 25 top meme reels punch in.

**How far: details and screens**
- To make something legible, zoom only as far as needed:
  - Code or small text: 1.5-2.2.
  - A dialog or panel: 1.4-2.0.
  - A full page: 1.0-1.3.
- Return to the wide view between major steps. Never hold one framing of a screen recording for more than about 4 s without a zoom move.

**Don't overdo it**
- Tie every punch-in or push to a reason: emphasis, a reveal or a detail. Moves at even intervals, or a zoom on every cut, read as a tic and annoy viewers. Follow the counts in edit-styles.
- Never transition out of a shot while it's pushing; let the move finish.
- Watch the source's own motion. A subject stepping back can cancel a push, and a subject moving fast makes a push feel queasy.

**Quality limits**
- The clip list gives each clip's sharp-zoom limit at this video's aspect ratio. Stay at or under it.
- Clips already enlarged to fill the frame (typically horizontal footage in a vertical video) get zoom 1.0 or at most a very gentle push.
- Don't zoom into heavily compressed footage.
