# Kinetic Type Brand Sting (motion-design showreel)

## When to use
Use this when the brief is a short, wordless brand piece: a creative school, agency, studio or app showing off its taste. Inputs are usually a profile or app screenshot, a logo, a few rendered or screen-recorded clips, and a track. Nobody talks to camera. The music is the script, and the on-screen words are lyrics or punchy fragments timed to the beat. If the footage has a person speaking, use a talking-head skill instead.

## What the reference shares (measured)
- Duration 15.5s. Target 14 to 17s and never exceed 20s.
- Speech share 0%, no voiceover. Audio is 100% original audio: one rap track carries the whole piece.
- 0.13 hard cuts/s, median shot 6.3s. The camera rarely cuts hard. Scene changes happen through continuous moves: a push into the phone, type flying past the lens, a fade to black. Expect about 6 visual sections, but only 2 to 3 should read as hard cuts. Join the others with zoom_in, dip_to_black or crossfade so the piece feels like one camera travelling.
- Palette: near-black frames with one saturated light source per section (electric blue, then pale grey, yellow/cyan, blood red, white). Every section has a vignette-dark edge.

## Structure recipe (15.5s)
1. **0 to 3.5s, product hook.** Show a phone or the brand's profile screenshot as a `card` overlay, centered, on a dark blue background. Apply a slow push from zoom 1.0 to 1.8 over the full segment, with the focus point reframed on the profile avatar or logo. Use no text, and let the beat land first. Transition out with `zoom_in` (add a `whoosh`).
2. **3.5 to 5.5s, intro ad-lib.** Put a short glowing word (e.g. "Ahh", a brand sound, a 1-word tease) as `hero` text on a dark blue frame. Use the `pop` animation and a cool look, contrast +20, saturation +25. Transition with `crossfade` (0.3s) or `zoom_in`.
3. **5.5 to 10s, kinetic lyric run.** Use a light grey or white field with black heavy sans text. Give each phrase 1 to 3 words ("yeah I" → "I feel like"). Swap text on the beat every 0.6 to 1.3s (one or two beats at ~90 BPM), using `slide_up` or `pop`. Add a slow push of 1.0 to 1.3 across the segment for drift. Exit with `dip_to_black` (0.2s).
4. **10 to 12s, color punch.** Use a black frame with `stacked` text in two colors (yellow top line, cyan bottom line), set big and glowing. Add a `hit` SFX on the first word. Increase saturation by about 30.
5. **12 to 14s, dark payoff line.** Use deep red `hero` or `serif` text on black, ideally `text behind the person`/object if the footage has a foreground element (bars, a silhouette, a product). Lower brightness to around -15 and raise contrast to around +25. Optionally add one `glitch` transition (0.2s) into the end card. Never use more than one glitch per piece.
6. **14 to 15.5s, logo end card.** Put the logo as `title_3d`, or as `hero` text with `fade`, in white on pure black with a soft glow. If the source is a still, make it a `hold` shot. Add a slow push from 1.0 to 1.1 and a `ding` or `hit` at the logo's first frame. Hold at least 1.5s and end on black.

## Pacing and cutting rules
- Snap every section boundary and every text swap to the musical beat, not to words. With no speech, use the beat grid (at 91 BPM a beat is 0.66s, a bar is 2.64s). Major section changes land on bar downbeats.
- Sections run 1.5 to 4.5s. The hook section is the longest (3 to 4s). The lyric run can be long because the text keeps changing inside it.
- Inside a section, text changes every 1 to 2 beats. Never leave the frame static for more than 1.5s: keep either a push or a text swap happening.
- Use speed 1x on renders. Use 0.5 to 0.75x only to stretch a too-short product clip, and never ramp above 1.5x.
- Use no more than 3 distinct transition types in one reel: zoom_in, dip_to_black, plus one accent (crossfade or glitch).

## Framing
- Keep every hero element dead center, inside the middle 60% of the frame. The edges stay dark.
- On screenshots or devices, reframe the focus point onto the logo or avatar so the push ends on the brand mark.
- Run the text big: 2 to 4 words should fill 70 to 85% of the frame width.

## Text and captions
- Do not run auto captions. There is no dialogue to time against, and transcription of sung lyrics is unreliable. Place text items manually on beats.
- Use heavy sans weights (`hero`, `stacked`). Use `serif` only for the dark payoff line, if you want contrast.
- Choose one color per section, with a glow feel: white or blue on blue, black on grey, yellow plus cyan on black, red on black, white logo on black.
- Allowed animations are `pop` (on hits), `slide_up` (in lyric runs) and `fade` (logo only). Use `typewriter` only for a URL or handle.
- Use `highlight words` on the single most important word per section.
- Keep explicit lyrics on screen only if the client supplied them. Otherwise write clean 1 to 3 word brand lines that scan with the rhythm.

## Motion templates
- Use `title_3d` for the end-card logo. This is the best match for the reference's lit 3D logo.
- `stat_card` is optional in section 4 if the brand has a number ("10k students"), styled to the section color.
- Do not use `character_intro`, `cast_lineup` or `lower_third_3d`. There are no people to introduce.
- Do not use graphics such as `follow_pill`, `comment_cta`, `progress_bar` or `emoji`. They break the premium dark look. The logo card is the only CTA.

## Look
Use an overall look of `cinematic`, or `punchy` if the footage is flat. Per shot, set brightness -10 to -20 on dark sections and contrast +15 to +30. Raise saturation only on the colored text sections. Never use `vintage` or `black_and_white`.

## Sound
- The track runs from frame 1 to the end at full volume, since there is no speech to duck under.
- Use sparse SFX, 3 to 5 total: a `whoosh` on zoom_in transitions, a `hit` on the color-punch and logo, and an optional `riser` over the last 1s before the logo. Keep SFX tight on the beat.
- End the music on a bar line with the logo. If the track keeps going, finish with a 0.3 to 0.5s `dip_to_black`.

## Avoid
- Fast hard-cut montages. More than about 0.3 hard cuts/s breaks the single-camera feel.
- Bright full-frame backgrounds, except the one grey lyric section.
- Captions burned along the bottom, subtitle bars, or `karaoke_box`.
- More than 4 words on screen at once.
- Starting with the logo. It is the payoff, so it only appears at the end.
- Going past 20s.

**Music and sound** (from the reference reels)
- If the client supplies a track or the footage has its own audio, keep the original audio at full volume for the whole piece and time all text to its beats and lyrics. Otherwise pick a dark, medium-energy hip-hop bed around 85 to 95 BPM. Use beat-thee (91 BPM) first; use spec-ops (120 BPM, dramatic) for a harder, tense feel, or kings-trailer (80 BPM) for an epic logo payoff. Set volume high, since there is no speech so nothing to dip under. Set the start point so a strong downbeat or drop hits within the first 0.5s, rather than the intro. Music enters on frame 1 and ends on a bar line at the logo, with a 0.3 to 0.5s dip_to_black to cover any tail.
- Library moods: hip-hop, dark, moody, trap, dramatic-suspense, cinematic, bass-heavy, urban, neon, confident.
