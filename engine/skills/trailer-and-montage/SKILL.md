Use this when most of the footage has no talking, and the video is carried by pictures and titles.

**Choose a spine**
- **Craft:** how something is made.
- **Transformation:** before and after.
- **Spectacle:** the most striking images.
- **Mystery:** reveal something gradually.
- **Journey:** going from place to place.

The spine sets the order, so never order shots by file name.

**Shape of a 30–45s piece**

| Section | Shots | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 1–3 | 2–4s |
| Setup | 3–5 | 7–10s |
| Escalation | 6–10 | 10–14s, shots getting shorter |
| Climax | 5–8 | 6–9s |
| End card | | hold 2.5–3.5s |

For 15–25s, drop most of the setup.

**Building to the climax**
- Build, then hold one calm "breath" shot, then a fast burst, then a final hold.
- A very short `dip_to_black` (0.1s) just before the biggest hit makes it land harder.
- A `dip_to_white` flash (0.1s or less) works only on true peaks. Overusing it looks amateur.

**Every cut changes something**
- Each cut changes at least one of: size, direction of motion, brightness, color or subject. Never put near-identical shots side by side.
- Never use the same shot twice. Never put three or more shots of the same kind or size in a row.
- No single scene or angle should take more than about 40% of the runtime.
- Put high-motion shots on the peaks and calm shots in the valleys.
- Never cover a moment the viewer should see with a transition or flash.

**Spoken lines in a montage**
- Keep 1–4 short lines (1–3s each) per 40s, with their sound.
- A line earns its place only if it creates mystery, states the stakes, makes someone human, or sets up the climax.
- A line right before a hit should end 0.2–0.6s before the cut.

**Self-check**
- **If it feels chaotic:** remove 20–30% of the shots, group shots by visual logic, and add a held shot before the climax.
- **If it feels slow:** shorten the setup.
- **Shot lengths:** no shot under 0.25s, and no shot over 6s except the final hold.
