Pick one style before planning, and keep its numbers across the whole video. Mixing styles is what makes an edit feel off.

**How to choose**
1. Follow the brand style rules or the editor's notes if they name a feel.
2. Otherwise go by format and content:
   - **Punchy:** high-energy social.
   - **Standard:** most social shorts and ads.
   - **Documentary:** testimonials, interviews and podcast clips.
   - **Cinematic:** premium brands and montage.
3. When in doubt, choose Standard. Most clients ask for "slower" far more often than "faster".

| | Punchy | Standard | Documentary | Cinematic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B-roll shot length | 0.8–1.5s | 1.5–3s | 3–5s | 3–6s |
| Gap kept between spoken phrases | 0.1–0.2s | 0.2–0.4s | 0.3–0.6s | as performed |
| Emphasis punch-ins | up to 1 per 5s, 1.12–1.15x | about 1 per 10s, 1.08–1.12x | about 1 per 30s, 1.05–1.08x, or slow pushes | slow pushes only, 1.00→1.05–1.10 |
| Non-cut transitions | up to 1 per 10s, short slides or flashes | 0–2 per video | crossfades at section changes only | crossfades and dips at act breaks |
| Words per caption (vertical video) | 2–3 | 3–5 | 4–6 | spoken lines only |
| Look | `punchy` or `warm` | from the brand | `natural` | `cinematic` or from the brand |

**Rules for every style**
- **Speech sets the length of a speaking shot.** Never cut someone off to keep the pace; the table's shot lengths are for B-roll.
- **Avoid a metronome rhythm.** Never use the same shot length three times in a row. Vary it, for example long, medium, short, short, LONG.
- **Fast cutting tires viewers after about 8s.** Put at least one held shot every 15s, longer than the shots around it, and place one right before the climax.
- **Tie every move to a reason.** Each punch-in, push or transition lands on an emphasis word, a number, a claim, a reveal or a section change. Evenly spaced moves read as a tic.
- **Never put a transition on a shot that is mid-push.** Let the push finish, then cut.
