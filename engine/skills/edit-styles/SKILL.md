Pick one style before planning, and keep its numbers across the whole video. Mixing styles is what makes an edit feel off. Comedy skits, series episodes and meme posts have their own measured profiles in `cutting` and their format skills; use those instead of this table.

**How to choose**
1. Follow the brand style rules or the editor's notes if they name a feel.
2. Otherwise go by format and content:
   - **Punchy:** high-energy social, creator reels, party recaps.
   - **Standard:** most social shorts and ads.
   - **Documentary:** testimonials, interviews and podcast clips.
   - **Cinematic:** premium brands and montage.
3. When in doubt, choose Standard. Most clients ask for "slower" far more often than "faster".

| | Punchy | Standard | Documentary | Cinematic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B-roll shot length | 0.45–1.5s (party footage down to 0.45s) | 1.5–3s | 3–5s | 3–6s, or one bar per shot on a 4/4 track (about 2s) |
| Gap kept between spoken phrases | 0.12–0.25s, nothing over 0.3s inside a shot | 0.2–0.4s | 0.3–0.6s | as performed |
| Emphasis moves | a punch-in or emphasis zoom about every 5–15s (1.3 on a wide, 1.3+ on a chest-up, a 1.5–1.6 zoom ramp at most every 10–15s) | about 1 per 10s, 1.08–1.12x | about 1 per 30s, 1.05–1.08x, or slow pushes | slow pushes only, 1.00→1.05–1.10 |
| Non-cut transitions | a short whip on each set-up change, or up to 1 per 10s | 0–2 per video | crossfades at section changes only | crossfades and dips at act breaks |
| Words per caption (vertical video) | 1–3 | 3–5 | 4–6 | spoken lines only |
| Look | `punchy` or `warm` | from the brand | `natural` | `cinematic` or from the brand |

**Rules for every style**
- **Speech sets the length of a speaking shot.** Never cut someone off to keep the pace; the table's shot lengths are for B-roll.
- **Avoid a metronome rhythm.** Never use the same shot length three times in a row; vary it, for example long, medium, short, short, LONG. Two exceptions: in a music montage cut on a bar grid, equal lengths of a whole number of bars are fine when every cut changes subject and size; and a one-item-per-shot format (a list, an outfit change) may keep equal item lengths.
- **Fast cutting tires viewers after about 8s.** Put at least one held shot every 15s, longer than the shots around it, and place one right before the climax.
- **Tie every move to a reason.** Each punch-in, push or transition lands on an emphasis word, a number, a claim, a reveal or a section change. Evenly spaced moves read as a tic.
- **A size change at a cut inside one take is 0 or at least 0.2–0.3.** Smaller changes read as a glitch.
- **Never put a transition on a shot that is mid-push.** Let the push finish, then cut.
