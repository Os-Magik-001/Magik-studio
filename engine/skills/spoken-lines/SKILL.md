---
name: spoken-lines
description: Writing lines people say naturally on camera or in voice-over, in French or English
agents: scriptwriter
---

- Write the way the person talks, not the way a brochure reads. Short sentences: 12 words or fewer, one idea each.
- Read every line out loud. If you'd stumble, cut it. If it sounds like an ad, make it sound like a person.
- About 2.5 words per second for a relaxed voice, 3 for an energetic one. Count: a 30 s video holds 60–80 spoken words, not 150.
- Leave silence where the picture tells the story (the crack of the crust doesn't need words).
- Numbers and names said the way people say them ("vingt-sept", "quatre heures").
- In Québec French, natural and warm; avoid France-French expressions and anglicisms the client wouldn't use. Keep "tu" or "vous" consistent with the brand.
- Non-actors (most clients) need lines that are easy to say: no tongue-twisters, no long lists, room to rephrase in their own words. Mark which lines can be improvised.
- Voice-over and on-camera lines are labelled so the videographer knows who says what, and where.
