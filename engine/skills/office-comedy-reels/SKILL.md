# Office comedy: episodes, POV sketches and meme posts

## When to use
Use this for scripted workplace or startup comedy: office scenes intercut with sit-down confessionals, first-person POV sketches, and short meme posts about startup life. With The Boyz, insta-series sets the series format (cold open, title, subtitles, specials) and this skill the comedy cutting. The `cutting` skill has the cut mechanics behind the numbers.

## What the most played reels do (measured, Oct 2026)
- **Cluely episodes** (23 most played, 4-21 M plays): 44-138 s (median about 62 s), 0.36 cuts per second (median shot about 2.1 s; dialogue singles 1.5-3 s), 135-224 words per minute, one handheld phone plus a fixed confessional set (a closet rail or a white door), in-camera pans and whips between speakers in 21 of 23, original audio, no music under dialogue, no sound effects, no grade, hard cuts only.
- **More than half the cuts fall inside continuous sound,** not in pauses: jumps or reframes inside one speaker's running speech, a confessional voice running over B-roll, sound-first entries into confessionals, speaker changes with no air. Only about a third are in silence.
- **The Marketing Interns** (0.6-14 M plays): 22 of the top 25 are no-dialogue meme posts of 6-25 s: a premise line on screen from frame 0, a song or trending sound as the whole soundtrack, 2-3 locked-off wides of the cast.

## Hook (first 2 s)
- **Episodes:** a POV line from frame 0 in 22 of 23 ("POV: your chopped coworker gets a girlfriend"): `on_screen_text`, two lines, small white sans, lower case, no box, high (12-25% from the top, 15 of 23) or mid-frame (40-55%, 8 of 23), held through the whole first scene (3-10 s, median about 5.5 s) and removed on the first new shot or confessional.
- Then either the first line within 0.3 s (the conflict already in motion; 11 of 23), or a silent action from frame 0 that is the premise (a hand in a drawer, the HR lady lighting a bin, a Ferrari reveal), for 3-7 s (6 of 23). Never dead air, and no establishing shot over about 1 s.
- **Series branding** (The Boyz): see insta-series.

## Structure recipes
**A. Episode (45-100 s; The Boyz 25-58 s)**
1. **0-5 s:** POV line plus the conflict or the premise action.
2. **Escalate in the scene** with handheld coverage: singles of whoever speaks, a two-shot for group beats, pans or whips between people when the camera moved.
3. **First confessional by 6.7-13 s** (median about 9 s). A flat name card on each character's first confessional (see Text).
4. **Middle: alternate scene and confessional.** The joke engine is the **claim contradicted by the next shot**: a confessional claim, then the scene that proves it wrong (or the reverse), the contradicting picture arriving 0.0-0.3 s before the key word.
5. **The button:** cut within 0.2 s of the button line (10 of 23), or 0.5-1.0 s after a confessional tag (5 of 23), or end on a silent 1.9-3.7 s reaction (6 of 23), or cut a line off mid-sentence as the character walks out. Callbacks to the opening make good buttons. No outro, no CTA voice, no fade.

**B. POV sketch (the camera is a character)**
- One jump per sentence or list item of the rant, 0.8-4.3 s apart, the topper on its own jump; whips between mini-scenes (cut inside them); the reply as the button.

**C. Cast / season intro (30-60 s)**
- Series: a `character_intro` per founder: freeze (`hold` 2.2-2.6 s, starting 0.0-0.4 s after his defining line), the person in colour over a darkened or grey background, a big name or archetype ("LE CEO"), then resume live; live stretches of 3-8 s between freezes; close on `cast_lineup` with the series title. A song near voice level may run under the whole reel and fill the freezes (the 10.9 M-play friend-types reel does).
- Meme-speed "meet the team": a claim card of about 1.5 s with the claim in quotes at the top, then the teammate posed still for 1.4-1.8 s with the roast line in a second colour at about 62% height, a `dip_to_black` on every change on a grid of about 2 s, mock-epic music at full level.

**D. Meme post (7-14 s, no dialogue)**
1. **Setup:** one static wide, `zoom` {1.0, 1.0}, `beat: setup`, 2.5-5.9 s (target 3-4.3 s), the premise line on screen from 0.0 s. No cut in the first 2.5 s.
2. **The turn** at 37-48% of the reel: on the frame the twist text appears when the new picture is the twist, otherwise 0.8-1.3 s after it, with the cast reacting in the last 0.2-0.4 s before the cut.
3. **Payoff:** 50-63% of the reel, labelled `punchline` or `reaction`: one held shot of 3-6 s, a borrowed reaction clip, or a ping-pong of 0.7-1.0 s shots ending on a pose held 1.3-1.5 s. End inside the payoff: no button, no end card.
4. **Cut points:** on movements (an exit while the last people are still in frame, the peak of a gesture, a phone lifting into its screen) and sound events (a voice onset or a phrase end within one frame, the music returning, a lyric gap), not on a beat grid (measured at chance).
5. **List meme:** the text changes every 1.0-1.5 s over one held shot, shortening toward the twist (1.5, 1.5, 1.0, 1.0), a 0.2 s dark beat on each change; the twist item stays to the end.

## Pacing and cutting rules (episodes)
- **Air: setup lines are crushed and the punch word gets a beat.** 0.15-0.3 s between lines on the same shot (median 0.23 s); speaker changes on a cut 0.0-0.25 s (about 0.1 s). Two shots joined by `say` keep about 0.3 s, which reads fine; for a rapid exchange from one take, keep both lines in one segment. Slower scenes keep 0.5-0.7 s at a speaker change.
- **Remove** fillers, stutters, retakes and empty gaps over about 0.75-0.9 s, not the breathing between lines.
- **The beat before a punchline: 0.8-1.6 s** (17 of the 23 most played keep 0.8-2.3 s), up to 2.3 s for a deadpan one-word reply ("No."), on a held face, a stare to the lens, an insert or B-roll ("Hmm" [0.9 s] "His smile."). Keep the setup's last phrase and the punchline in one segment labelled `punchline` and write "beat" in its `purpose` ("punchline, 1.1 s beat before"): the trim pass then leaves the segment as planned. Or split the take into two contiguous segments: a silent `reaction` segment (empty `say`) covering the pause, then the `punchline`. No punch-in on that pause.
- **After the punchline:** 0.0-0.3 s, then cut away: to the listener on the last syllable (a 0.1 s L-cut is fine) or straight into the next setup. Never hold on the speaker.
- **Reactions:** silent reaction shots 1.4-2.5 s (median about 1.9 s; 15 of 23): with no laugh track, the hold is the laugh. A final or shock reaction runs 1.9-5.3 s. Three reaction shots in a row feel right; four is too many (reactions interleaved with inserts can run longer). If you punch in at all, do it here: a static 1.07-1.25, at most about one per 30 s; escalate 1.0 → 1.24 → 1.66 only on a victim's face under a long line of bad news.
- **Instant reply:** when the joke is how fast someone answers, jump out the listener's turn so the reply starts within 0.1 s of the question.
- **Confessional with B-roll** (the voice runs longer than the face in 15 of 18 episodes): one confessional segment carries the whole `say`; show the face 1-4 s at the start (median face piece about 2.5-2.8 s), then lay `cutaway` overlays from 0.0-0.3 s before each key word of the claim, 0.6-3.3 s each (median 1.3 s; 0.7-1.0 s in a list; a rule-of-three at 0.83-0.93 s each), and end them 0.0-0.2 s before the tag line, so the punchline is on the face (`beat: punchline`). One uncut 6-9 s confessional per episode is fine when it holds the main joke and its pause.
- **Sound-first entries into a confessional** (15 of 23, the house signature): the confessional's voice starts 0.3-2.7 s (typically 1.0-2.5 s) before its picture. Start the confessional segment early and cover its first 1.0-2.5 s with a `cutaway` of the scene's continuing picture. Into a new scene, its first line may start 0.2-0.7 s before its picture (10 of 23); an off-screen interviewer's question may finish 0.6-1.8 s over the subject sitting still. Exits from a confessional are straight cuts onto the next scene's first sound (0.0-0.2 s).
- **Jump cuts in monologues, rants and lists:** at each sentence or list item, the pause removed completely, the size kept (9 in 10 change under 3%). A size change of 0.08-0.2 reads as a glitch; use 0 or at least 0.3.
- **Shot length:** dialogue singles 1.5-3 s; a handheld take with continuous dialogue and a moving camera may run uncut to 12-15 s (10 of 23 do), a confessional with an off-screen question to about 11 s; a static shot over 6 s needs a question, a reaction or a camera move.
- **Inserts:** 1.0-2.3 s on the named object, cut on the word or into a 0.3-0.5 s gap with the voice running over it (19 of 23); a smash cut to "the thing just named" can recur as a running cross-cut.
- **Silences** of 3-11 s with no dialogue only for physical action or a payoff insert, cut into shots of 0.5-1.8 s on the action.
- **Split edits** in scene dialogue are automatic (4-6 frames) and match the references; longer ones are built with cutaways (above).
- **Speed:** keep 1x. **Transitions:** `cut` (inside in-camera whips when the footage has them).

## Framing
- 9:16 vertical. Reframe on the speaker's face with eyes at about 35-40% from the top.
- In two-shots, reframe toward whoever delivers the line. Hold the wide for physical gags and group gags (memes never punch in).
- Confessionals stay centred, static and framed head to knees, in one fixed set reused for every character.
- Overall look `natural`. If a shot is dim, add brightness +0.1 at most and keep saturation untouched.

## Text and captions
- **Subtitles:** `captions.mode: auto` (or `translate` for French or franglais), preset `minimal` or `subtitle`, white, small, no box, lowercase as spoken, 2-4 words per screen (median 3), at about 75-80% of the height (22 of 23). Correct names from the script but keep the speaker's own grammar ("show me picture"). Star the swears ("sh*t"), shouts in CAPS.
- **Highlights:** none, or at most one per 15 s on the punchline word.
- **Name cards** (14 of 23, never in an episode 1): at each character's first confessional only: bold white name over a small white role ("Ling Long / Systems Engineer", "Tim / Marketing"), flat, no box, at the left, 57-78% of the height, fading in 0.1-0.6 s after the cut, held for that first face piece (1-4 s). A `label` pair; `lower_third_3d` is the 3D version.
- **No captions** on meme posts: the premise line carries the text (top band 12-25% from the top over a wide, mid-frame over empty floor, under the face on a close-up; quotes for a line said to the cast, "pov:" for a situation; kept to the end or swapped on the payoff cut in the same place and style).
- Use `card` for screenshots the joke relies on (a leaked chat, a salary sheet, a Slack message), upper half, 2-3 s while the line referencing it plays.

## Motion templates and graphics
- `character_intro` and `cast_lineup`: intros and season openers only. `stat_card`: an absurd number gag ("Runway: 11 days") for 1.5 s.
- No `comment_cta`, `follow_pill`, `progress_bar` or emojis inside skits: they break the mockumentary illusion. A single 💀 at the end of a meme's premise line is allowed.

## Music and sound
- **Episodes:** original audio only, at full level; no bed under dialogue or confessionals; no sound effects. Music sections only under silent action (about 10 of 23): the hook, a chase, a mock-hero walk, a montage, a pathos ending; they cut dead on the next line or on a black.
- **Mock-epic stakes** (a fire drill, a pitch to investors) may use a `low` dramatic bed under the action, cut dead on the punchline.
- **Meme posts:** `audio: mute` on every segment and the track as one `music` section from 0.0 to the end at `high`, started so a hit or the first vocal lands on the first action (0.6-0.9 s). It is the soundtrack, not a bed.
- Borrowed clips (a film moment, a meme reaction) are the payoff of many memes; the engine can't use them, so build the payoff from the cast's own reaction and say so in `open_questions`.

## Avoid
- Bouncy word-pop captions, karaoke boxes or big centre captions on dialogue.
- Colour grades, vignettes, and transitions between scenes.
- Music under confessionals and scene dialogue.
- Punch-ins on punchlines, and size changes of 0.08-0.2 at jump cuts.
- Leaving setup air before the first line, holding on the speaker after the punchline, explaining the joke with text after it lands.
- Ending on anything except the laugh.
