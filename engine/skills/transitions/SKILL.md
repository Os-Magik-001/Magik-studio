**Hard cuts are the edit.** In measured top-performing reels, about 95% of transitions are hard cuts. A transition is a punctuation mark: it signals a change of time, place, section or energy. If you can't name what it signals, use a hard cut.

**Budget**
- Social shorts and ads: at most 2 eye-catching transitions (slides, wipes, zooms, flashes) per video.
- Crossfades and dips at real section changes don't count against that budget.
- Documentary, interview and news pieces: hard cuts, plus 0.5–1s crossfades or dips only at act breaks. No slides, wipes or zooms.
- A cut used as a rhythm beat (on an action, a word or a hit) must be a true hard cut. Even a short dissolve blurs it.

**Which one**
- **`crossfade`, 0.4–0.7s:** time passing or a change of place, in calm pieces.
- **`dip_to_black`, 0.3–0.5s:** a chapter break, or a breather before a new section. Use at most 2 per 30s. It pairs well with a title on the next shot. Use 0.1s just before a big hit in a montage.
- **`dip_to_white`, 0.1–0.2s:** a flash on a true energy peak or reveal. Overusing it looks amateur.
- **`slide_left` or `slide_up`, 0.25–0.35s:** high energy into high energy, like a whip pan. Hold at least 0.7s of each shot on either side. Use one direction for the whole video (`slide_left` reads as "forward"); never mix them.
- **`zoom_in`, 0.3–0.4s:** going into a detail of what was just shown.
- **`wipe_left` and `circle_open`:** novelty moves. Use them only when the brand style is explicitly playful.

**Rules**
- Use one kind of transition per video, two at most.
- Never put a transition on a shot that is mid-push; let the move finish first. Never hide a moment the viewer should see under a transition.
- A transition overlaps both shots by its length. Keep it well under half of either shot.
- Before the end card: a hard cut, or a 0.3–0.5s `crossfade` or `dip_to_black`.
- Never start the video with a transition.
