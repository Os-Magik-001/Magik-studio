---
name: talking-head
description: Editing someone speaking to camera so it looks like a studio interview or a pro creator reel, not a selfie
formats: all
style: dynamic
---

Use this whenever a clip is mostly one person speaking to camera, in any format. The `cutting` skill has the cut mechanics; creator-reels has the full creator recipes.

**Timing of the voice**
- Social talking heads keep 0.12-0.25 s between phrases and cut every pause over about 0.3 s inside a shot (measured on the studio's creator and realtor references, 200-240 words per minute). Interviews and calm brand pieces keep 0.3-0.5 s.
- Cut at sentence ends with 0.1-0.15 s after the last word and about 0.1 s before the next when the set-up changes; inside one set-up the picture may change between two words of a running sentence.
- End 0.0-0.4 s after the last word. Never on a trailing "so yeah".

**Sizes from one camera**
- Alternate a wide framing (zoom 1.0) and a tighter one at phrase changes, roughly every 2-4 s: 1.3 on a full-body or wide source, 1.3-1.6 on a chest-up source (within the clip's sharp-zoom limit). This is what turns a jump cut into a deliberate second angle.
- A size change at a cut inside one take is either 0 or at least 0.2-0.3. A change of 0.08-0.2 reads as a glitch: the studio's clients sent back cuts at 1.0 / 1.1 / 1.15.
- A same-size jump is fine between two sentences, or when a card, a hero title or a 1-2 frame white flash lands on the same frame.
- On held shots, a slow push from 1.00 to 1.05 adds life without being noticed.
- **Emphasis zoom (social reels):** on the key line or number, zoom about 1.5-1.6 in 0.3-0.5 s, hold 1-2 s, and come back in 0.5-0.7 s, at most once per 10-15 s. Build it from three contiguous segments of the same take (`zoom` {1.0 → 1.6}, {1.6 → 1.6}, {1.6 → 1.0}), `focus` on the face. For calm interviews keep the small static emphasis punch-in (1.08-1.15), rare.
- Set `focus` on the face so the eyes sit in the upper third. In vertical sources the face usually sits 20-35% from the top.

**Several set-ups**
- With footage from several rooms, outfits or angles, change set-up every 4-8 s and never put two takes from the same set-up and framing back to back; change the size at every set-up change. A set-up change hides a cut-out take better than any punch-in.

**Keep the speaker on screen**
- The face should be on screen at least 65% of the runtime; the measured realtor and creator references keep it close to 100% and show what the speaker names as graphics on her shot instead of B-roll.
- Show a named thing as a card or graphic on the word (within 0.1-0.3 s), one every 2-5 s. Full-screen `cutaway` B-roll: at most 3 per minute, each 1-2.5 s (a proof screen 3-3.5 s), with at least 1.5 s of face between two cutaways. A cutaway can cover a jump cut in the middle of a sentence, because the voice carries on underneath.
- Short evidence beats work well in 20-40 s pieces: two or more full-screen shots of the result, product or proof, returning to the speaker between them.
- Leave blinks and quick glances (under 0.5 s) alone. Only hide a real look-away, cheapest fix first: trim it, then change size, then a cutaway.
- On the pivot lines where the argument turns ("but", "here's the thing"): in a social reel, give the pivot the biggest title and optionally a 0.1-0.2 s white flash; in an interview or a calm brand piece, give the speaker a clean full-frame moment with no text.

**Grade: keep the speaker as shot**
- Faces are where grading goes wrong. Keep the speaker close to the original: `natural` look, or a gentle `warm`.
- Use corrections only within brightness -0.05 to +0.05, contrast 1.0 to 1.05 and saturation 1.0 to 1.06, unless the shot is genuinely badly exposed.
- Match cutaways to the speaker, not the speaker to the cutaways.
- Avoid `cinematic`, `vintage`, `black_and_white` and `punchy` on talking heads, unless the brand style asks for them.

**Text around a speaker**
- Never put text over the face, from the eyes to the chin.
- Captions sit over the chest area (`center` or `bottom`). Titles go at `top`, or behind the head (`behind_subject`) when it fills the upper frame.
