---
name: talking-head
description: Editing someone speaking to camera so it looks like a studio interview, not a selfie
formats: all
---

Use this whenever a clip is mostly one person speaking to camera, in any format.

**Framing rhythm**
- Alternate a wide framing (zoom 1.0) and a close framing on each cut, like a two-camera studio interview.
- The close framing can go as tight as the clip's sharp-zoom limit allows; the clip list gives it. 4K footage can go to about 1.9 on a vertical video, while 1080p should stay subtle.
- Set `focus` on the face, a little above center of the head and shoulders, for example y 0.35 to 0.45. Then a punch-in keeps the eyes in the upper third.
- On longer shots, a slow push from 1.0 to about 1.05 over the shot adds life without being noticed. Prefer this over frequent hard punches in calm or premium pieces.

**Cut on the words**
- Cut between sentences or on a natural pause, never mid-word (see picking-takes).
- Cutaways help pacing and hide cuts. Show what the speaker is talking about (a product, a place, a process) for 1 to 2.5s between lines.
- Each shot plays its own sound, so the speaker's voice can't continue under a cutaway yet. Place cutaways in the gaps between sentences, never over a line.

**Text around a speaker**
- Never put text over the face, from the eyes to the chin.
- Short lead-in captions read best centered over the chest area (`center`, or `bottom` if the face sits high in the frame).
- Use `top` only when the head is low in the frame.

**Grade**
- Keep skin natural. Dark or heavily graded looks push faces red or grey.
- `natural` or `cinematic` (gentle) suit most speakers, with `adjust` to keep the speaker brighter than the background if the room is dark.
