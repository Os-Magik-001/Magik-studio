---
name: talking-head
description: Editing someone speaking to camera so it looks like a studio interview, not a selfie
formats: all
style: dynamic
---

Use this whenever a clip is mostly one person speaking to camera, in any format.

**Two camera sizes from one camera**
- Alternate a wide framing (zoom 1.0) and a close framing (about 1.3–1.6, within the clip's sharp-zoom limit) at phrase changes, roughly every 2–4s. This is what turns a jump cut into a deliberate second angle.
- Without a size change, use a static punch-in of at least 1.05 on one side of the cut, or place the cut exactly where a title appears.
- On held shots, a slow push from 1.00 to 1.05 adds life without being noticed.
- Emphasis punch-ins stay small (1.08–1.15) and rare (see punch-ins-and-reframing). Don't zoom on every cut.
- Set `focus` on the face so the eyes sit in the upper third. In vertical sources the face usually sits 20–35% from the top.

**Keep the speaker on screen**
- The face should be on screen at least 65% of the runtime. Viewers stay for the person.
- Use at most 3 cutaways per minute, each 1–2.5s, with at least 1.5s of face between two cutaways.
- Use `cutaway` overlays for B-roll: the speaker's voice carries on underneath, so a cutaway can cover a jump cut in the middle of a sentence.
- Short evidence beats work well in 20–40s pieces: two or more full-screen shots of the result, product or proof, returning to the speaker between them.
- Leave blinks and quick glances (under 0.5s) alone. Only hide a real look-away, cheapest fix first: trim it, then punch in, then a cutaway.
- On the pivot lines where the argument turns, give the speaker a clean full-frame moment with no text.

**Grade: keep the speaker as shot**
- Faces are where grading goes wrong. Keep the speaker close to the original: `natural` look, or a gentle `warm`.
- Use corrections only within brightness -0.05 to +0.05, contrast 1.0 to 1.05 and saturation 1.0 to 1.06, unless the shot is genuinely badly exposed.
- Match cutaways to the speaker, not the speaker to the cutaways.
- Avoid `cinematic`, `vintage`, `black_and_white` and `punchy` on talking heads, unless the brand style asks for them.

**Text around a speaker**
- Never put text over the face, from the eyes to the chin.
- Captions sit over the chest area (`center` or `bottom`). Use titles at `top` only when the head is low in the frame.
