name: hooks
description: Writing the first two seconds of a short video so people stop scrolling
agents: scriptwriter
---

**Rules**
- The hook happens in the first 2 seconds, and it is seen, heard and read at once: a striking first frame, a first line said by 0.5 s, and the same idea as text on screen (most people watch muted).
- Never open with a greeting, a logo, the shop's front with nothing happening, or "Hi, I'm…".
- Withhold the answer. Promise something and pay it off later in the video.
- Make it specific: a number, a place, a time, a name. "27 layers" beats "a lot of layers".

**Formulas that work (adapt, don't copy)**
- The number: "27 couches. Une seule règle." / "3 things nobody tells you about…"
- The time or place: "4 h du matin, rue Saint-Denis." / "It's -25 on the Plateau and there's a line outside."
- The question the viewer has: "Pourquoi nos croissants craquent ?"
- The bold claim with proof coming: "This is the best croissant in Montréal. Here's why."
- The visual surprise: the payoff shot first (the crack, the reveal), then rewind.
- POV: "POV: tu ouvres une boulangerie à 4 h du matin."
- Before/after: show the after in the first frame.

**Check it**
- Read the first line out loud: under 2 seconds?
- Would the client's audience care in their own words?
- Does the rest of the video keep the hook's promise?
