# Sound for motion

**How many**
- About 10 effects in a 40–60 s reel, each tied to an object's move (a landing, a strike, a light, a camera pass). Never one per graphic. Comedy: 0–3. Captions, labels that track a part, small badges: none.

**Which**
- A distinct sound for each kind of move, and never the library effects used in the last delivery to the same client.
- Recorded, physical sounds: air, glass, paper slides, soft clicks, low felt hits, short swells. No cartoon sounds (bubble pops, boings, buzzers, bells, scratches).
- An effect lasts the move's length ±20%. Never one whose tail is cut off mid-ring.
- A whoosh has three layers: air, a tonal body and a texture. Effects with a pitch sit in the track's key (its root or fifth). Under a loud bed (above about −18 dBFS), fewer effects.
- A sound that repeats (list lines, ticks) rotates two or three takes: the same file on every hit is the most common tell.

**How loud**
- Peaks at -8 to -16 dBFS, with the voice at -16 LUFS; 4–5 dB lower under speech. Without a voice, each hit sits 3–6 dB over the music bed.
- A bed with most of its energy under 150 Hz plays as nothing on a phone speaker: check the bed on a phone-speaker band (300 Hz–8 kHz).
- The effects bus ducks 8 dB under any voice (12 ms attack, 200 ms release), not just under transcribed words.
- Measure it: render the effects stem and read each cue's peak against the voice around it.

**When**
- A whoosh starts 0.1–0.15 s before the move and peaks on its fastest frame. An impact's peak lands on the landing frame or one frame after, never before: light arrives before sound, and an early sound feels wrong sooner than a late one (HyperFrames). A swell ends on the moment it announces.
- No impact on a spoken word. Under speech, the cue moves to the nearest breath between words (at most 0.3 s early or 0.2 s late). Cues tied to a picture event (a flash, a landing) stay on their frame.
- The logo's sound rises only after the last word.

**Check**
- Transcribe the master and compare it with the clean voice: any word that changes or drops under an effect means that effect moves or gets quieter.
- Never an effect on a punchline.
