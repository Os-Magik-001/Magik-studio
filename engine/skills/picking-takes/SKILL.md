description: Choosing the best take of each line and trimming speech cleanly

**Rebuild the script from the takes**
- People repeat a line until it lands, so the footage often holds several takes of the same sentence. Use the transcript to match takes to the script's lines.
- Use the **last complete, clean take** of each line: no stumble, no restart, no trailing off. The last one is usually the one the speaker was happy with.
- Pick an earlier take only when it is clearly better, or the editor's notes point to it ("the better section starts at 0:47" means one continuous take from there).
- Never splice half a sentence from one take onto half from another. The voice, energy and position won't match.

**In and out points around speech**
- Start about 0.05 to 0.1s before the first word, and end in the silence right after the last word.
- Never clip the first or last syllable.
- Transcript word times can be 0.1 to 0.3s off, and a transcript can show a word at a cut that isn't really there. Leave a small margin rather than cutting tight to a word time.
- Cut out dead air between sentences: pauses, "um", false starts, the breath before a restart. Keep a natural beat (about 0.15 to 0.3s) where a pause carries meaning or lets a point land.

**Cutting inside one camera angle**
- When two shots in a row come from the same clip and angle, change the framing on one so the cut reads as intentional. Use a static punch-in (see punch-ins-and-reframing) or alternate wide and close.
- A raw jump cut with no framing change looks like a mistake.

**Flag what you couldn't fix**
- If no clean take of a line exists, use the best one and say which line in `open_questions`.
