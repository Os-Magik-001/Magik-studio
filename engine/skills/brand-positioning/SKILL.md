**The niche, in one line**
- Category + what makes it specific + where: "artisan bakery-café on the Plateau, Montréal", "premium barbershop for curly hair, Laval". A niche nobody could search for ("food business") is useless to the researcher.
- Keep the client's own words for what they do. Add the place when they sell locally; most of the studio's clients do.

**The position**
- Answer three questions from the questionnaire, in plain words: who it's for, what they get here that they don't get elsewhere, and why to believe it (a fact: since 2019, 4 a.m. baking, 300 clients, a signature product).
- Name the one thing the videos should make people remember. One, not five.
- Find the proof the camera can show: a process, a place, a person, a result, a number. Positioning that can't be filmed doesn't help the videos.

**Hashtags and accounts to study**
- 4 to 8 hashtags from broad to specific: one category tag, one or two local tags, the product tags, one community tag (e.g. boulangerie, montrealfood, croissant, plateaumontroyal).
- Accounts: the ones the client named first, then local competitors and the best-known accounts in the niche. Only accounts you are sure exist; never invent a handle.

**Watch out**
- Don't copy a competitor's position; find the gap they leave.
- Don't promise what the client can't deliver on camera every week.
