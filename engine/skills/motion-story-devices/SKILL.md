# Story devices for designed films

For films we design rather than cut from a talk: Magik's own promos, app and website launches, explainers, recaps. Talking-head reels follow hooks and short-form-script-structure. Distilled from HyperFrames' story notes (Apache-2.0), October 2026. Most weak films are good motion around no story: a list of features, three cards in a row, a logo, an address.

**The one thing**
- Say it in one line: « vous pouvez maintenant… » for a launch, the one idea for an explainer. Write what the viewer should remember in their words, not the slogan.
- One viewer doing one real task is the spine, and one real piece of data (a booking, a photo, a price) travels through it from the hook to the payoff. An explainer's spine is one concrete example worked through.
- What the source doesn't say stays unsaid: « 40 min à 2 min » is not « 20× plus rapide ».

**Three storylines, then one**
- Write three different films, each with its own world (where it happens and what it looks like), device (what the story is) and form (how the camera and the cuts carry it). Make one of them the bold one. For each: the first two seconds exactly, the beats in one line, the look in one line, the sound in one line. Pick the story a stranger would follow, not the most striking picture; recommend one and keep the other two in the brief.
- Devices: a moment in someone's day; a character does the work; the feature as the hook; the result first, then a pull back to where it came from; one object across apps; life before and after; type carries the argument; a number lands; a question with the input in it; the object is the explanation.
- Forms: one continuous take; an object-to-object chain (every shot ends on the thing that opens the next); an endless zoom into a detail that turns out to be the next world; a flight through one world; cuts on the beat; match cuts on one held shape; a split screen collapsing to one; one frame that keeps rebuilding itself. Three storylines that are all plain cuts haven't tried the forms.

**The beats** (a 30–40 s promo; the device may bend it)
- Hook, about 8%: the world and the need, or the feature already working. Frame 0 is the thumbnail: never black, never a logo alone.
- Tension, about 12%: the old way failing on screen (the wait, the wall of text, the wrong result).
- Reveal, about 8%, 20–30% in: the product or idea named.
- Proof, about 40%: the real task as 3–6 verbs, one beat each, keeping only what the hand or the cursor touches. The process runs fast.
- Payoff, about 20%, 60–80% in: the output arrives already moving; the biggest visual moment, once, at normal speed.
- Claim, about 8%: the one thing in one line. End card, about 8%: the mark carried in, the name and the call to action holding 2–3 s.

**Worlds**
- Several worlds, each with its own background, light and scale, never one dark canvas with small things floating in it. Enter each through an object already on screen (designed-transitions). Keep one home stage to return to, one carrier the eye follows, and the same palette and type throughout.

**Copy**
- At most 6 words a line (an explainer's up to about 10), one line at a time, no full stop at the end. Show, don't list: no bullet slides or three-card rows; similar things each get their own picture.
- Never open on « Découvrez » or « Introducing »; never « révolutionnaire », « sans effort », « game-changer », « en quelques secondes » unless it was measured.
- A voiceover is never repeated word for word on screen. Read the lines alone: a stranger gets the pitch. Watch the pictures alone: they tell the same story.

**Explainers: pick the shape first**
- Start where the viewer is (a person, a situation, their question); the device enters as the answer, and the film comes back to the person at the end.
- One diagram that grows a part per beat as each is named, never dropped whole; or continuous transformation (a bar becomes an axis, a dot becomes a crowd).
- Shapes of information, so that similar lines get different pictures: a number that lands, a myth struck through, before and after, many converging into few, a network filling in, one chosen out of many, steps in order, a real photo with the detail circled, a quote set as a poster, a chapter card.

**The brand**
- The mark lands once, with weight, on the end card; before that the brand lives inside the story, never docked small in a corner. The accent is rationed: one highlighted word, the payoff, the end card.

**Tells of a generated film**: a dark void with small floating things; glow and particles as decoration; the cream paper, serif and mono-label look; everything fading and sliding up; three cards in a row; a docked logo; typing into a prompt box as the opener; side-by-side panels standing in for a scene.
