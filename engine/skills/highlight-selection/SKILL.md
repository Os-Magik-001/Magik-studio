---
name: highlight-selection
description: Finding a complete, standalone moment in long talks, interviews and podcasts
formats: podcast_clip, social_short
---

Use this when the footage is much longer than the video, for example a talk, an interview or a podcast episode.

**Find complete ideas, not keywords**
- Read the whole transcript first and list every self-contained viewpoint or story before choosing.
- A good clip holds three things: the question or subject, the reason or example, and the conclusion.
- End after the conclusion lands, not at the first pause.
- Never cut a viewpoint short to hit a target length. A complete 50s clip beats a broken 30s one. Say in the summary if you went over the target length.
- Reject a moment that only works with context the viewer doesn't have. Don't lower the bar just to produce something.

**Boundaries**
- Don't start on a word that points back to earlier talk: "and", "but", "so", "because", "also", "then", or a pronoun like he, she, it, they or that whose meaning came earlier.
- Don't end on a dangling word: "and", "but", "because", "of", "for".
- If the clip still needs one line of context, open with a title that gives it ("On why most startups fail:").
- Leave about 0.2s before the first line and about 0.3s after the payoff, so the clip doesn't start or end abruptly.

**Quote hook** (optional, for social)
- Open with the single strongest line from inside the clip, the one that is surprising, useful or a clear benefit.
- Follow it with a short `dip_to_black` (about 0.3s), then play the clip from its real start, so the line plays again in its place.
- Only do this when the line makes sense on its own. Mention it in the summary.

**Rank candidates by**
1. Makes sense on its own.
2. Strength of the hook.
3. A clear payoff.
4. How much it matters to the viewer.
5. Visual interest.

The first two matter most.
