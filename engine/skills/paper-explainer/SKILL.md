# Paper explainer

Distilled from Charlie Hills' vox-explainer skill (MIT, October 2026). Use it when a client explains a « why » (why the skin ages, why a treatment needs three sessions, why prices went up) and a 3D world (one-world-explainer) would be too heavy or too cold.

**Script**
- 8–14 shots of one sentence each. Shot 1 is the question itself, said big; the first surprise comes within 5 s.
- Every shot answers something the line before it said. If you cannot say what a shot proves, cut it.
- The last shot is the takeaway in five words or fewer.
- Every name, date and number on screen has a source in the brief. Say « ressemble à », not « c'est », when the source only shows a likeness; « une raison », never « la raison ».

**The look**
- Torn-edge paper cards with small, tight shadows on a paper ground; halftone dots, paper grain and fine film grain over the whole frame; a soft vignette. Colour fringing at the frame's edges only, never on the text.
- The brand's palette (a red, a cream, a navy and one yellow accent is the classic one), bold condensed type set as solid blocks (Anton, Bebas Neue, Oswald).
- Motion on twos: every pose holds 2 frames at 24 fps (in a 30 fps edit, update the drawing at 12–15 poses per second). Never smooth corporate easing in this look. No typewriter text, glow, bounce or gradients on type.

**Shot types**
- **Statement**: three short lines of bold type on a torn strip, the line you want remembered.
- **Cards**: several things at once (products, quotes, before photos) dropping in on torn cards, a beat apart.
- **Before / after**: the same card, a date chip that flips.
- **Call-out**: a marker circle draws itself around one thing in a photo, then its label.
- **Counter**: a number counting up in code, never an estimate.
- **Grid**: many identical tiles, then one breaks the pattern (« ils se ressemblent tous »).
- Cards and photos that move in depth, never a lone bar or line sliding across the frame (it reads as static). Equal card sizes and gaps: a lopsided grid reads as a mistake. A logo fills its card.

**Sound**
- The voice sits on top. Under it: a low bed, a paper or soft impact on each new shot, a lift under the last line (motion-sfx-sync). If the voice is hard to hear, lower the score.

**Checks**
- One frame from the middle of every shot: no cut-off text, no card over another card's text, every name and date matching the brief character for character, nothing smooth that should be stepped.
