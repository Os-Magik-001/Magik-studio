name: retention
description: Keeping viewers watching vertical social video from the first frame to the end
formats: social_short, ad
style: dynamic
---

**Frame 0**
- The first frame is the strongest shot, showing the subject, the speaker or the premise.
- Never open with a fade from black (0.1 s of black at most), a title alone over black, a logo, or an establishing shot of a building with nothing happening.
- Put the hook text on screen from 0.0 s, not after the first line: most people watch muted. In the studio's references it is there in 25 of 25 top meme skits, 22 of 23 top Cluely episodes (the POV line) and 6 of 7 creator reels. When the first line is spoken by 0.2 s with word captions, its captions already serve as the hook title (the top restaurant reels work this way); add a separate title when the voice starts later or there are no captions.

**The hook, by format** (measured on the most played references)
- **Talking creator reels:** the first word by 0.25 s, the hook claim against what the viewer believes ("before you hire a realtor, don't just take their word for it"), the first cut or size change within 0.25-2.6 s; one cut per part of a three-part hook (3 cuts in 2.3 s in the fastest reel).
- **Dialogue skits:** the POV line from frame 0 and either the first line within 0.3 s or a silent action that is the premise, for up to about 5 s; the first cut around 3 s.
- **Meme skits:** a held, composed wide with the premise line, no cut in the first 2.5 s; the twist cut at about 44% of the reel.
- **Montages and event promos:** the name of the place or event within 1.2 s, the first full-frame shot on the drop; a burst of 3-5 very short shots (0.3-0.6 s) previewing the payoff works for montage content.
- **Series episodes:** a 2-3 s cold open before a title of at most 2.5 s; a 5 s title before the first word loses the scroll.
- Write hook titles that withhold, not ones that close the question: "This $4 tool replaced my whole kit" beats "Here are 5 tools". Keep any number, hold back the answer, add who it's for when useful ("for small cafés"). If two openings make the same promise, keep one.

**Keep something changing**
- Something visible changes at least every 2-4 s in talking reels: a cut, a size change, a new caption, a hero word or a card. The top creator reels change their text 17-20 times per 10 s.
- Counted together, cuts and other changes should come about once a second in punchy edits. A cut that matches a reference's cut rate but has no other changes will still feel slow.
- In dialogue scenes, a static framing over 6 s needs a question, a reaction, a size change or a camera move. A continuous camera move under a running voice, or a payoff the viewer is watching, counts as a change.
- The flat middle is where people leave. Put a punch-in, a cutaway, a card or a title there.
- When the speaker says a re-hook line ("but here's the crazy part", "it gets worse"), keep it, and mark it with a size change, a title or a short flash.
- Cut the dead frames: walk-ins over 1.5 s, empty air over 0.5 s before a scene's first line, reactions running past their peak. In the studio's own series they took 5-25% of each episode.

**Ending**
- End on the payoff. Talking reels end 0.0-0.4 s after the last word; skits within 0.2 s of the button line or on a silent 2-3 s reaction; memes inside the payoff, with no button.
- If the brand kit or the editor's notes say no end card, or ask for a loop, set `end_card.include` to false. Make the last shot echo the first shot's framing, so a replay feels seamless. Loops work best at 7-15 s; a longer reel can loop by ending on its opening shot with the narration's last sentence unfinished, so the first line completes it.
- Never end on dead air, a fade or a trailing "so yeah".
