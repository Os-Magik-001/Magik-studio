---
name: retention
description: Keeping viewers watching vertical social video from the first frame to the end
formats: social_short, ad
style: dynamic
---

**Frame 0**
- The first frame is the strongest shot, showing the subject or the speaker.
- Never open with a fade from black, a title alone over black, a logo, or an establishing shot of a building.
- Put the hook title on screen from 0.0s, not after the first line. Most people watch muted.

**The hook**
- Cut the opening 2–3s roughly twice as fast as the body, or open on one striking action.
- A burst of 3–5 very short shots (0.3–0.6s) previewing the payoff works for montage content.
- Write hook titles that withhold, not ones that close the question:
  - "This $4 tool replaced my whole kit" works better than "Here are 5 tools".
  - Keep any number, and hold back the answer.
  - Add who it's for when that's useful: "for small cafés".
- If two openings make the same promise, keep one.

**Keep something changing**
- Something visible changes at least every 2–4s: a cut, a punch-in, a new caption or a title. A stretch of 6s or more with no change feels dead.
- Counted together, cuts and other changes (new captions, titles, punch-ins) should come about once a second in punchy edits. A cut that matches a reference's cut rate but has no other changes will still feel slow.
- The flat middle is where people leave. Put a punch-in, a cutaway or a title there.
- When the speaker says a re-hook line ("but here's the crazy part", "it gets worse"), keep it, and mark it with a punch-in or a title.

**Ending**
- End on the payoff, then the end card.
- If the brand kit or the editor's notes say no end card, or ask for a loop, set `end_card.include` to false. Make the last shot echo the first shot's framing, so a replay feels seamless. Loops work best at 7–15s.
- Never end on dead air or a trailing "so yeah".
