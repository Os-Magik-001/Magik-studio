**Slow motion (speed 0.4 to 0.6)**
- Use it for one hero moment: a pour, a splash, hair moving, a product landing, a smile.
- It only looks smooth on footage shot at 50 fps or more; the clip list gives each clip's frame rate. On 24–30 fps footage it stutters, so don't go below 0.75, or skip slow motion.
- Mute the sound (`audio: mute`): slowed-down sound rarely helps. Keep it only if the brief wants the moment to feel dreamlike.

**Speed-ups (speed 1.5 to 4)**
- Use them for process and the passing of time: setting up, walking through a space, assembling something, a long pour.
- Speeding up keeps the pitch natural, so you can keep the sound at up to about 1.5x.
- Above 1.5x, mute speech, since sped-up words sound wrong. Ambient sound can stay.
- Never speed up someone speaking to camera, with one exception. If the editor's notes ask for faster delivery from a slow speaker, 1.1x on the talking shots, sound kept, tightens them while keeping the pitch natural. Never go above 1.15x on speech.
- Slow motion never goes on a speaking face.

**Ramp effect**
- To build a ramp, put shots from the same take back to back: a normal or fast shot, then the key moment at about 0.3–0.5x for at least 1.3s on the timeline, then back to 1x or faster.
- The contrast between the speeds is the energy beat.
- Use a ramp at most once per video.

**Timing**
- A shot lasts (end - start) / speed on the finished video.
- Plan caption timing on the finished timeline, not on the source clip.
