# Coverage, cutaways and inserts

This is how to cover a voice and build a scene from shots, based on 267 reels. Cutaways are plain cuts, so this also fits the clean style. Everything here uses `cutaway` overlays, shot order and quoted words.

1. **Set the pace by format.**
   - Talking piece from one setup: 0.25-0.35 cuts/s (a change every 3-4 s), cut at sentence ends.
   - Demo, list or proof-heavy reel: 0.6-0.8 cuts/s (1.2-1.6 s shots).
   - Candid rant or one-take performance: one shot, zero cuts.
   Don't mix rates within one section.
2. **Cut to what is named.** When the voice names a concrete thing (an object, a place, a document, an action), put a cutaway over the voice. Put the in-point on that noun's first word and the out-point at the end of the phrase, 1-2 s in all. A picture that matches the word lands instantly; a generic B-roll shot doesn't.
3. **One shot per repeated pointer word.** On 'this, this and this', 'here, here, here' or 'like this, like this', give each repetition its own cutaway, snapped to that one word. Order them from weakest to strongest, so the last one is the most striking.
4. **Lists and numbered points.** Each item, or each 'first / second / third', starts a new shot or insert. Put the in-point on the item or number word. Return to the speaker between groups, not after every item.
5. **Keep the face for the payoff.** Never cover a punchline, the CTA or the sign-off with B-roll. Cutaways go on setup lines. The last line of a beat returns to the speaker.
6. **Bridge locations with detail.** To move from one place to another, use this sequence:
   - a wide at place one;
   - a medium;
   - a wide as the move starts;
   - 2-4 close-ups of about 1 s each (door handle, keys, feet, wheel);
   - a wide at the new place.
   With only one close-up available, use one 1-2 s extreme close-up (or a punch-in to 2.5 on a detail) at the change. Hard cuts throughout.
7. **Proof pairs.** Show the result first, then how it was made. The result is labelled setup; the reveal ('I shot this on my phone', the BTS) is labelled punchline. For a before/after, cut on the word 'this' in 'from this to this'.
8. **Cold open on the best exchange.** In interviews and Q&As, take the question and the shortest, most surprising answer from later ('Very.', 'I was so bad'). Use them as the first two shots (setup, punchline), then the intro. This is the clean-comedy cold open applied to talk formats.
9. **Reactions from off camera.** For a laughing host, a partner, or a crew member interrupting, label the shot reaction and cut to it on the quoted line. The automatic L-cuts and J-cuts handle the overlap; don't time it yourself.
10. **One deliberate break per reel.** A self-interruption that resets attention ('Cut!', greeting the dog, 'Step... I lost count') can stay as its own shot. Quote its words so trimming keeps it, and label it other (or punchline if it is the joke). In the clean style, keep it only when it plays as character, never a slate or crew talk.
11. **Cut on the movement gap.** When changing angles on a walk or a turn, end shot A at the word gap where the stop or the look happens. Start shot B on the delivered line, so the motion carries the eye across the cut.

## Example
Voice: 'I've tried this tripod, this one, and this one. None of them worked. Here's the one I use.'
Shots:
- A-roll on 'I've tried'.
- Cutaway on 'this tripod' (0.8 s), cutaway on 'this one', cutaway on 'and this one'.
- Back to the speaker for 'None of them worked.' (punchline, not covered).
- Product insert on 'the one I use'.
Why: one picture per pointer word, the joke line stays on the face, and the reveal gets its own insert.

## Example
Interview, 90 s:
- Shot 1: the host's question from 1:12 (setup).
- Shot 2: the guest's 'Honestly? Terrible.' (punchline).
- Then the series intro, trimmed to 2-3 s, then the conversation.
