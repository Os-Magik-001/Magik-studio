**Length** (set it by format; measured on the studio's references)
- When the script doesn't say: 20-35 s for ads, reviews and visits.
- Meme skits 7-14 s; creator talking heads 25-55 s; comedy episodes 25-60 s (Cluely-style episodes up to 100 s); event recaps 15-45 s; contests and street games 55-70 s; announcements and giveaways 45-50 s.
- For an upper bound like "under 30s", land a couple of seconds under it. For a 60 s platform limit, plan 58 s.

**Choose the spine of the cut**
- **Voice-led** (narration, a review, an announcement, a creator talking): the voice runs on and the pictures change on it, in the gaps between words, each named thing arriving 0.1-0.5 s before its word.
- **Dialogue-led** (a skit, an interview, a street game): cuts on the speakers' lines and reactions; on a group wide, same-size jump cuts remove the hesitations.
- **Music-led** (a montage, an event recap, a meme): cuts on movements, actions and the music's phrases (see trailer-and-montage).

**Structure**
1. **Hook, 0 to 2s.** The most striking moment or the most intriguing line, the product in hand or in action, something moving in the first second. Text on screen from frame 0 unless the first spoken line's captions are already up by 0.2 s.
2. **Payoff early, 2 to 8s.** Show what the video promises: the product, the result, the reveal, the prize.
3. **Middle.** Deliver the value, about 70% of the runtime:
   - Change something every 2-4 s: a shot, a size, a set-up, a card, a caption.
   - Include one hero moment: a held shot of the dish, the reveal, a slow push.
   - Put a re-hook (a line, a size change or a title) where the energy dips.
4. **Close.** End on the strongest image or line. For a local business, the address or the sign on screen for the last 2-3 s (11 of the 18 top restaurant reels); otherwise the end card, or a loop if the brand prefers no end card.

**Pacing**
- B-roll and montage shots: follow the chosen edit style, usually 1.5-3 s; food named in a spoken list 0.5-0.9 s per item; hero shots 1.3-2.8 s; information (date, address, offer) held 2-3 s.
- Talking-to-camera stretches run as long as the speech. Hide cuts with set-up changes (another room, another speaker), framing changes (see talking-head) or, on a group wide, same-size jump cuts; not with long unbroken takes.
- A continuous camera move under a running voice counts as a change.

**Look and text**
- Captions on, one line, sized for a phone: 1-3 words for fast narration and creators, 2-4 for dialogue.
- Big title words only on spoken numbers, prizes and the brand for restaurant and venue clients; hero words every 2-3 s for creators.
- Look: from the brand. `punchy` or `warm` suit energetic B-roll; stay `natural` on talking heads.
- Transitions: hard cuts inside a scene, one designed transition per change of place or section (see transitions).
