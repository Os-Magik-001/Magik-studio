## Conflicts with the current skills

- Opening shot: Adrian says 'always start on a wide establishing shot'. clean-comedy-edit and office-comedy-reels say open cold on the conflict within the first second, with no establishing shot. The studio rule is right for skits and reels, because a scenic opener loses the scroll. The two are compatible only when the first shot is a wide framing (zoom 1.0) that already carries the conflict line. A silent establishing wide is allowed only in wordless montages.
- Grade: Adrian grades everything to a warm cinematic look, darkened from the phone default and graded with a LUT. clean-comedy-edit and office-comedy-reels require look natural and allow only exposure fixes (brightness up to +0.08/+0.1). The studio is right for comedy skits, because the handheld mockumentary feel depends on an ungraded look. The cinematic/warm look and darkening belong to dynamic non-skit reels only.
- Caption style: Adrian uses editorial, small, yellow serif, lower-middle, 3-5 words per screen. clean-comedy-edit sets subtitle preset, bottom, 6-8 words; office-comedy-reels sets minimal, lowercase, 2-4 words at about 68% height. The clients' presets stay. One part of Adrian's lesson should be adopted: if the 'bottom' position renders inside the lowest 20-25% of the frame, the platform username and caption cover it, so clean-comedy-edit should move to lower-middle, just above that band.
- Punch-in depth: Adrian alternates wide 1.0, medium 1.4-1.6 and close 1.8-2.5. clean-comedy-edit limits tighter framings to 1.2-1.35. For the studio's 1080p phone footage the studio limit is right, because a 2x crop visibly softens faces. Deeper punch-ins only on 4K sources or detail inserts.
- Flubs and bloopers: picking-takes and clean-comedy-edit say never keep a flub. Adrian keeps 'Cut!', fumbled restarts and dog interruptions as gags, sometimes labelled 'NEVERMIND'. The studio rule is the default. A flub stays only when it plays as character or is the joke, as in clean-comedy-edit's own 'je vais construire' example: at most one per reel, never a slate or crew talk.
- Joke annotations: Adrian adds asterisk labels like '*TALKING ABSOLUTE GIBBERISH' and '*CONCERNED'. office-comedy-reels forbids explaining the joke with on-screen text after it lands, and clean-comedy-edit forbids all such text. In clean skits, office-comedy-reels is right. In dynamic skits, allow one label, only as the punchline itself, landing with the reaction and never after the button.
- CTAs and end cards: Adrian ends most reels with comment_cta, follow_pill or a stacked promo end card. office-comedy-reels bans CTA graphics inside skits, and clean-comedy-edit ends on the button with no outro. The studio is right for skits. CTAs belong to non-skit creator or tutorial reels only.
- Sound effects: in one breakdown Adrian puts a sound effect under every action and every text pop. office-comedy-reels allows only a whoosh into character_intro and a hit on the freeze, and clean-comedy-edit allows none. The studio rule is right, and Adrian's own later 'less is more, only on peaks' lesson agrees with it.
- Never wide-to-wide: Adrian forbids cutting wide to wide on talking shots. clean-comedy-edit says comedy often plays best in the wide. Adrian's rule applies to a single-speaker monologue from one angle. In dialogue scenes, a wide-to-wide cut onto a different speaker or a different camera is fine, and the wide should be kept for physical gags.
- Composition in two-shots and eye height: Adrian balances two-shots on the center line and places eyes at the upper third (33%). office-comedy-reels reframes toward whoever delivers the line, with eyes at 35-40%. Use a center-balanced two-shot for setups. Shift onto the speaker's lead eye at x 0.5 for punchlines. Keep eyes at 33-38% so both rules hold.

## What the engine can't do yet

- **Plain or solid background plate behind floating cards (cream, white, grid paper, dark grid)** (55 reels): A background generator (solid color, vignette or texture) that a card can sit on instead of footage
- **Split screen: before/after halves, final-over-BTS stacked panels, two synced clips side by side** (34 reels): A two-panel layout (top/bottom or left/right) with independent clips or independent grades per half, plus a moving wipe divider
- **Advanced color: custom LUTs, log-to-Rec.709 conversion, curves/HSL, masks or windows, film grain, halation, glow** (22 reels): LUT loading, color-space transform, per-channel and hue curves, shape masks, and grain and halation effects
- **Circular picture-in-picture facecam over screen recordings or examples** (20 reels): A card overlay with a circle mask and position control
- **Blur: blurred background outros and text that resolves from a blur** (16 reels): A per-shot Gaussian blur and a blur-in/blur-out text animation
- **Letterbox or fit-to-width widescreen inside the vertical frame (also 4:3 and postcard borders)** (14 reels): An aspect matte or fit-to-width layout with black (or custom) bars and optional rounded corners
- **Drawn annotations: center guide line, 3x3 grid, tinted safe-zone overlays, arrows, scribbles** (14 reels): Line, grid, arrow and region-tint graphics, ideally with hand-drawn stroke animation
- **Ambience, foley and custom audio layers (room tone, crowd, car, user-recorded sounds)** (12 reels): An ambience and foley library and import of user audio files, with per-layer volume
- **Dialogue processing: noise reduction, EQ, compression, de-essing, speech enhance and mic matching** (9 reels): A voice-processing chain beyond loudness matching (denoise, EQ, compressor, enhance)
- **Keeping retakes, bloopers and repeated words on purpose (outtake montages, 'today, today, today')** (9 reels): An explicit per-line override of retake grouping, so earlier takes and false starts can be kept in order
- **Cutting and phrasing to the music's beat** (7 reels): Beat and downbeat detection on the music bed, with an option to snap cuts and text pops to beats
- **Per-speaker caption colors (host yellow, co-host pink)** (7 reels): Caption color assigned per speaker from diarization
- **Card styling: rotation, tilt or 3D perspective, drop shadows, stacked prints** (7 reels): Card transform controls (rotation, perspective) and a shadow or border style
- **Multi-clip grids and mosaics of past episodes or guests** (6 reels): A grid layout of several simultaneous clips, optionally scrolling
- **Generative fixes: video generation for missing shots, generative extend, restyle, generative fill for vertical backgrounds** (5 reels): Generative video and image models in the pipeline
- **Export control: ProRes or high-bitrate codec, uncompressed audio** (5 reels): User-selectable export codec, bitrate and audio format
