# @cluely: what the office-comedy series teaches the edit engine

**Covered:** all 141 reels up to 150 s posted 2025-07-04 to 2026-10-21 (146.8 min), measured automatically. The 23 most played (4.2 to 20.8 million plays) were watched frame by frame (hook at 4 fps, the whole reel at 2 fps, 8 frames around every cut); 3 of them are reposts of the top 3 (same cut points), so they hold 20 distinct edits.

**What the account is:** Cluely's office series ("Ep 1" to "Ep 28", written and directed by @remyzeee): 18 mockumentary episodes with talking-head confessionals against a white door or a closet rail, 4 POV sketches where the camera is a character, and 1 sibling sketch, with recurring characters (Brandon, Ling Long, Roy, Tim, Jason). The studio owner's model for cutting comedy, including The Boyz.

## How the top 23 are cut

### The pace
- **0.36 cuts per second, median shot about 2.1 s** (per-reel medians 1.27-3.75 s); lengths 6.3-137.4 s (median 62 s); 135-224 words per minute. That is about 40% faster than the numbers in the current skills (0.26 cuts/s, 3.1 s median shot).

### What the cuts land on: mostly inside continuous sound
- Over the 215 cuts measured in ranks 9-16: 32% in silence, 14% on the first or last sound of a phrase, 54% inside running sound. Checked by eye, the cuts inside running sound are:
  - **one voice running over B-roll or an insert,** a confessional or narration continuing while the picture changes, often mid-word (18 of 23): DOT3nOCFBZO @ 12.47, DQnWkAsD284 @ 6.30, DaoWtGpp5-e @ 11.27-17.30;
  - **jump cuts inside one speaker's running speech** at a comma or sentence end, the pause gone (16 of 23; 31% of all cuts in ranks 9-16): DXdBtTfM2ai @ 44.67 / 45.90 / 46.93, every 1.0-1.2 s;
  - **speaker changes with no air:** DUHEiWjksNJ @ 23.40 cuts 0.02 s before the new speaker's first sound; 12 of Dd9bmKepccj's 31 cuts are turns with the gap removed;
  - **a reaction over a line that continues** (the investor holding his nose over the jargon, DXdBtTfM2ai @ 48.83);
  - **bridges between scene and confessional** (see J/L).
- **Cuts in silence** land on a scene change, an insert, a physical action or a return to a face; the next line then starts 0.15-0.9 s later (median about 0.39 s).
- **Cut on action** in 14 of 23: the hug (DO4WD7IkiHs @ 3.53), the shirt lift (DSG1AeYiWap @ 1.40), climbing out of the pool (DRa1Lc1DwRh @ 6.33), a hand pushing at the lens (Dd9bmKepccj @ 20.23).
- **The last syllable of a punchline is a cut point:** the picture leaves within 0.0-0.3 s of it in every reel where it was measured (DO4WD7IkiHs @ 35.83, 0.03 s after "penis"; DbMixlZJJoo @ 43.00, 0.04 s after "ignored"), to the listener, the proof or straight into the next line.

### Timing: setup lines are crushed, the punch word gets a beat
- **Gaps:** between phrases inside a shot median 0.16-0.26 s; at a speaker change on a cut 0.0-0.25 s (about 0.1 s).
- **A pause of 0.8-2.3 s before a punchline in 17 of 23:** 1.85 s on the thinking face before "Tuesday" (DOT3nOCFBZO @ 3.33-5.18); 1.53 s before "Also, he is a gay." (DUHEiWjksNJ @ 46.37-47.90); 2.27 s of blank faces before the deadpan "No." (DXdBtTfM2ai @ 85.0-87.36). The pause sits on a held face, a stare to the lens, an insert or B-roll, never on a punch-in.
- **Confessionals often open on a silent stare of 0.55-1.04 s** before the first line (DSG1AeYiWap @ 29.07).
- **The opposite device:** setup exchanges and "instant reply" gags with no air at all (9 of 23): Tim interrupting 0.19 s after Jason (DSG1AeYiWap), the rejection after 0.26 s (DOT3nOCFBZO @ 34.85), "Yeah, for sure" right after "Go away. Go away." (Dax18eXBzCP @ 29.30).

### J/L cuts (confirmed by eye)
- **Only about 6 of the 58 J/L labels checked are split edits between speakers;** the rest are one voice running across picture changes. In-scene speaker changes sit on the boundary ±0.13 s, which the engine's automatic 4-6 frames already reproduce.
- **The house signature is the sound-first entry into a confessional (15 of 23):** the confessional's voice starts 0.3-2.7 s (typically 1.0-2.5 s) before its face, over the last shot of the scene (DUHEiWjksNJ @ 35.53, 0.72 s; DXdBtTfM2ai @ 52.77 and @ 75.03, 1.6 and 2.5 s; DWkFUsQJkYK @ 34.13, 2.25 s, the denial heard over Tim chugging). Voice-first passages run 3.5-4.8 s of voice over B-roll before the face.
- **The next scene's line heard before its picture** in 10 of 23 (leads 0.17-1.1 s, median about 0.47 s); **an off-screen interviewer's question finishing over the confessional subject** (0.6-1.8 s) in at least 6; **exits from a confessional are straight** (0.0-0.2 s onto the next scene's first sound); **a sentence split between scene and confessional** in 3 ("my elbow hurts a little bit, | but thank God...").

## Rules for the engine (comedy skits in the Cluely style)
1. **Setup exchanges are tight:** speaker changes 0.0-0.25 s (target 0.1 s), lines on the same shot 0.15-0.3 s (target 0.23 s). The engine's fixed handles leave at least 0.32 s between two `say` shots; keep rapid exchanges from one take in one segment.
2. **Pre-punchline beat:** keep 0.8-1.6 s (up to 2.3 s for a deadpan one-word reply) on a held face, an insert or B-roll: one segment with the setup's last phrase and the punchline, `beat: punchline`, "beat" in its `purpose`; or a silent `reaction` segment covering the pause followed by the `punchline`. Never punch in during the pause. After the punchline 0.0-0.3 s, then cut to the listener, the proof or the next line.
3. **`reaction` segments:** 1.4-2.5 s when silent (target 1.9 s), 1.9-3.7 s as a final button, up to 5.3 s for a shock beat. Cut to the listener on the last syllable of the absurd line (an L of 0.1-0.25 s is fine).
4. **Confessional = one segment carrying the whole `say`:** the face for the first 1-4 s with the name card, then `cutaway` overlays of 0.6-3.3 s each (median 1.3 s; 0.7-1.0 s in a list; three of 0.83-0.93 s for a rule of three), each cut in 0.0-0.3 s before the word it illustrates or contradicts, ending 0.0-0.2 s before the tag so the tag is on the face. One uncut 6-9 s confessional per episode when it holds the main joke and its pause.
5. **Sound-first entries:** into a confessional, start the confessional segment 1.0-2.5 s early (range 0.3-2.7 s) with a `cutaway` of the scene's last shot over that lead; into a new scene, start its first line 0.2-0.7 s before its picture; an off-screen question may finish 0.6-1.8 s over the subject sitting still; exits from a confessional are straight cuts (0.0-0.2 s).
6. **Contradiction cut:** a hard `cut` 0.0-0.11 s after the claim's last word into the proof, or the proof as a `cutaway` starting 0.0-0.3 s before the claim's key word.
7. **Jump cuts in rants, lists and monologues:** at each sentence or item, the pause removed completely, every 0.8-4.3 s; the topper on its own jump; `zoom` unchanged (or within 0.02-0.06); a jump may skip a movement so the reply is instant.
8. **Punch-ins:** at most about one per 30 s, static 1.07-1.25, on a `reaction` or a listener; not on the punchline.
9. **Inserts:** 1.0-2.3 s on the named object, cut on the word or into a 0.3-0.5 s gap with the voice continuing.
10. **Shot lengths:** reel median about 2.0 s; dialogue singles 1.5-3 s; a handheld take with continuous dialogue and camera moves may run 12-15 s; a static shot over 6 s needs a question, a reaction or a camera move.
11. **Silence:** 3-11 s with no dialogue only for physical action or a payoff, cut into 0.5-2 s shots on the action or held as one take; a smash cut to about 1.5 s of black and silence may follow the biggest physical climax.
12. **Text:** subtitles 2-4 words, lowercase, small white, no box, at about 75-80%, swears starred, shouts in caps; the POV line from 0.0 s until the first new shot or confessional (3-10 s, target 5 s), high or mid-frame; a flat name card on each character's first confessional (left, 65-75%, 0.1-0.5 s after the cut, held for that face piece).
13. **Hook:** the first word within 0.3 s, or the premise as silent action from frame 0 under the POV line for up to 7 s; never dead air, no establishing shot over about 1 s.
14. **Button:** cut within 0.0-0.2 s of the button line (within 1.0 s of a confessional tag), or a silent `reaction` of 1.9-3.7 s, or a callback; never a line after the topper.
15. **Music:** none under dialogue or confessionals, and no effects; music sections only under silent action (the hook, a chase, a montage, an ending), cut dead on the next line or a black.

## Against the current skills
- **Gaps** (office-comedy-reels and clean-comedy-edit: 0.25-0.45 s on the same shot, 0.5-0.7 s at a speaker change, "under 0.2 s feels rushed"): measured 0.16-0.26 s and 0.0-0.25 s at 190-224 wpm. The references are right for this house; the sitcom figures suit slower scenes such as The Boyz.
- **The pause before a punchline** (clean-comedy-edit and the trim pass: 0.45 s; pauses over 0.75 s deleted): 17 of 23 keep 0.8-2.3 s. The references are right; the current numbers delete the best beats.
- **Reaction holds of 0.6-1.2 s:** the references hold 1.4-2.5 s, up to 3.7-5.3 s at the end or on a shock.
- **Punchline punch-ins** (office-comedy-reels 1.0 → 1.3 on the punchline; clean-comedy-edit 1.2-1.35): punchlines get a new shot; punch-ins are rare, 1.07-1.25, on reactions. The trim pass's 0.14 punch-in on cut pauses should be 0-0.06.
- **Confessional length "3-8 s, then cut back":** the face is on screen 1-4 s while the voice runs 3-14 s over B-roll, with one 6-11 s uncut piece per episode.
- **"Hard-cut on the last word" for the contradiction:** the proof arriving 0.0-0.3 s before the key word is as common.
- **"Never exceed 9 s without a cut or punch-in":** 10 of 23 hold handheld takes of 9-15 s with a moving camera or running dialogue.
- **"First spoken word within 0.3 s":** only 11 of 23; 6 open on 3.3-7.3 s of silent premise action. Both work.
- **POV line "top third, held 2.5-3.5 s":** held 3-10.5 s (median about 5.5 s), high or mid-frame.
- **`lower_third_3d` at about 60% for 2.5 s:** Cluely uses a flat name and role at 57-78%, held for the first face piece (1-4 s), in 14 of 23. The owner decides whether The Boyz keeps 3D.
- **clean-comedy-edit "6-8 words per screen, sentence case, no name cards":** 2-4 words, lowercase, at 75-80%; a POV line in 22 of 23 and name cards in 14.
- **Split edits "automatic, 4-6 frames":** right in-scene, but the signature splits are 0.3-2.7 s sound-first entries into confessionals (15 of 23) and 0.2-1.1 s pre-laps into scenes (10 of 23), built as planned `cutaway` leads.
- **"End on the laugh, no outro":** confirmed for 15 of 23; add the silent-reaction button (6 of 23).
- **"Three reaction shots in a string, four is too many":** holds for consecutive reactions; reactions interleaved with inserts can run long (10 faces and 11 screens in a silent investigation, DaoWtGpp5-e).
- **Confirmed:** hard cuts only, no grades, no effects, original audio, no music under confessionals, one fixed confessional set, physical-gag silences of 3-11 s, a mock-epic bed under the action only, stumbles kept when they are the character.

## What the engine can't do yet
- **In-camera pans and whip pans** between characters (21 of 23): no animated `focus`, and slides or glitches are not a whip; footage shot this way must be kept as whole takes.
- **Gapless exchanges** (0.0-0.1 s at a cut), because of the fixed 0.12 / 0.2 s handles.
- **Long sound-first entries and pre-laps** without hand-built `cutaway` overlays; the automatic split edits are 4-6 frames.
- **Per-speaker caption colour** (yellow for the off-screen voice), caps for shouts, and hand-corrected subtitles keeping non-native grammar (that needs the script, not the recogniser).
- **Camera-made gags in POV sketches** (the door shutting on the lens, the camera "blown back" across the room) and props (spit-takes, flames, a CCTV laptop screen).
