# @kapsalon.mtl: what a local restaurant's reels teach the edit engine

**Covered:** all 66 reels up to 150 s posted 2022-03-22 to 2026-10-08 (30.6 min; one longer reel left out), measured automatically. The 18 most played (11.9k to 55.8k plays) were watched frame by frame (hook at 4 fps, the whole reel at 2 fps, 8 frames around every cut).

**What the account is:** a halal kapsalon and burger restaurant in Montreal, in French and Québécois. The 18 watched: 5 contests and street games (56-70 s), 3 announcements and events, 6 narrated reviews and visits (25-42 s), 3 sketches, 1 set of customer testimonials. A prospective client of the studio.

## How the top 18 are cut

### Two cutting systems
- **Voice-led reels (10 of 18): pictures laid on an unbroken voice.** The picture changes in the gap between two words, never inside a word, and the voice never stops for a cut: DeP9o_JuTRA has 0 of 63 cuts mid-word, each centred in the word gap (0.24 s after the last word, 0.24 s before the next); CyVzzJSNVPv has 22 of 22 cuts inside speech.
- **The thing named arrives on or just before its word** (13 of 18; after it only once): for one named thing 0.0-0.5 s before (median about 0.25 s): the avocado 0.39 s before "avocats" (DeP9o_JuTRA @ 16.57), the menu 0.07 s before "étudier" (DAegRtHoSml @ 5.77), the oven 0.8 s before "four" (C3oAH0HtOlN @ 13.10); in an ingredient list 0.5-1.5 s ahead.
- **Dialogue reels (games, interviews, sketch dialogue; 6 of 18): a cut on the first sound of each reply,** speaker changes in the breath gap, 0.1-0.3 s of air in total (C1z9f2sNW8h 0.10 s before and 0.11 s after, 17 exact onsets; DAegRtHoSml 0.20 / 0.09 s). Pauses are removed with jump cuts at the same size.
- **Sketches cut after the line, in silence, with air** (3 of 18): 0.11-1.84 s after each call (median 0.56 s) in DDCyJAYP3wO; the comic card in DPCotWRkqWz comes 0.87 s after the line.
- **On action** (8 of 18): mid-movement (a hand pressing, a throw, a door's swing), the new shot entered 0.1-0.3 s after the movement starts; true match cuts in 3 (the cheddar held up, then dropping on the patty, DDsm7QrsqyJ @ 21.00; the fork rising, then into her mouth, C1FonGkrqrc @ 16.40).
- **On the beat** only in 2 B-roll runs (DDsm7QrsqyJ 8 of 21 cuts, 0.38 against 0.21 by chance; DeP9o_JuTRA 24 of 63); no dialogue cut sits on a beat.

### Shot lengths
- **Median shot over the 18: about 1.2 s** (0.63 to 3.5): under 1 s in 6, 1-2 s in 8, over 2 s in 4.
- **Pace does not predict plays here.** The two most played reels have the longest shots (3.0 and 3.5 s median; 13 and 6 shots); the two fastest (0.63 and 0.68 s) rank 16 and 10. The prize, the sketch premise and the people do the work.
- **Food named in a spoken list: one item per shot, 0.5-0.9 s** (DeP9o_JuTRA @ 15.27-23.53, median 0.68 s; C3oAH0HtOlN: 17 shots in 11.5 s). **Food hero shots 1.3-2.8 s.** **Information beats (date, address, offer) held 2-3 s** while the food runs at 0.5-0.7 s.
- **Shots shorten into a climax** (DDsm7QrsqyJ @ 21-32.9: 1.7-2.5 s down to 0.67-1.03 s as the bun goes on). One long moving take may replace cuts (an 11.8 s handheld orbit under the voice, Cm0Q_PlheUN @ 13.33).
- **Talking takes in sketches, testimonials and the top reel run 2-7 s**; the top reel (a PS5 contest) changes place between takes instead of framing.

### Jump cuts and sizes
- **Jump cuts in 9 of 18,** at the same size (x0.95-1.05), one every 0.5-2.1 s, the sentence running across (C1z9f2sNW8h @ 42.57 / 43.00 / 43.67). **No reel alternates wide and close on every jump.**
- **Emphasis punch-ins of x1.07-1.17 that return to the wide after 0.24-1.4 s** (4 of 18): on the stakes (C1z9f2sNW8h @ 24.23, x1.14), on "UN JEU ??" (DAzZySKINDH @ 21.77), on "c'est délicieux" (DAegRtHoSml @ 51.40). Larger only for a hook reveal (x1.33) or a comic snap (x2.2 for 0.27 s, DBkHxYKIt0F @ 7.60).
- **The subject's own movement does the push** in 4: the presenter walks to the lens (DPCotWRkqWz @ 0.0), food shoved at the lens.

### Inserts, reactions and tasting
- **The voice continues over every insert** (0.7-2.8 s), back to the speaker for the verdict word (DAMpBiSoen8 @ 11.77, back on "C'était incroyable" @ 13.13).
- **Food shots carry motion:** a hand acting or the camera moving in 8 of 18; several angles of one dish in a row in 3; food alternating with eaters' faces in 3.
- **Reactions are short singles, 0.5-1.1 s,** cut 0.0-0.55 s after the line, chained after a win (applause 1.06 s, the winner's "oui oui" 0.46 s: DAegRtHoSml @ 40.77-42.47).
- **The first bite keeps a silent chew of 0.55-0.87 s before the verdict** (C3oAH0HtOlN @ 23.13, DPCotWRkqWz @ 28.1), and the tasting or verdict is kept whole (2.3-3.4 s), usually the longest shot of its section.
- **J/L cuts:** none of about 30 checked is a two-speaker split edit; all are one voice running over a cutaway or a new angle of the same speaker.

### Transitions and interrupts
- **Designed transitions in 13 of 18, nearly all whip or zoom blurs, on section changes:** "place à la vidéo" (C1fPH0it0Co @ 8.27), "Voici KapSalon" (DeP9o_JuTRA @ 7.53), main course to dessert (C0Ucv4yNFH2 @ 20.83). About one per 15-23 s in games and announcements; one per change of space (every 5-6 s) in the house's narrated reviews. Hard cuts only in 5 of 18.
- In-camera transitions in 4 (a whip on a door's swing, a push into the burger until it blurs, a jacket filling the lens). White flashes in 3, never on a dialogue cut (into the address, into a milkshake).
- **Full-frame text cards as comic or time beats** in 3: "75 Years Later" (2.42 s), "30 secondes plus tôt..." (1.0 s), a black card "Il a pas joué." 0.03 s after the line.
- Verdict marks: ✓/✗ stickers in 5; a red or green full-frame tint of 0.5-0.7 s in 1. **Not used in any of the 18:** freeze frames, crossfades, speed ramps, slow-motion hero beats.

### Text and hooks
- **Word-timed captions in 12 of 18,** 1-3 words per screen (1 word for fast narration), 15-20 changes per 10 s, bold with an outline, uppercase in 9, yellow in the house's voice-over style; at 45-70% of the height between faces, 70-82% for one person or food. **Captions coloured per speaker in the games** (4 of 18: host yellow, guest green).
- **Big title words only on spoken numbers, prizes and the brand** (5 of 18: "PS5!!!", "25", a yellow "4"), about 0.5-1 s.
- **Hook title separate from the captions at frame 0 in only 3 of 18, exactly the three with no word captions;** in 8 others the first spoken word's caption is up by 0.0-0.15 s and acts as the title. The two most played reels show no text at frame 0 and open on a strong action (walking at the lens with the PS5; 2.7 s of silent bag-packing).
- **First word by 0.2 s in 9 of 18;** frame 0 shows the product in hand or in action in 8; something moves in the first second in 14. Cold opens mid-line or after the event in 4.

## Rules for the engine (local food and venue clients)
1. **Voice-led reels:** keep the narration in long segments and lay every picture on it as a `cutaway`; never end a segment just to change the picture. Start each cutaway in a word gap 0.1-0.5 s before the word that names its subject (aim for 0.25 s; up to 1.0 s in an ingredient list), never inside a word. Keep the voice's own pauses at 0.1-0.3 s: split a take at a pause over 0.35 s.
2. **Cutaway lengths:** 0.5-0.9 s per item in a spoken list (aim 0.7 s); 1.3-2.8 s for a hero dish or the product held up; 2.0-3.0 s for the date, address or offer with the text on screen. Food clips start 0.1-0.3 s after a hand starts acting (pour, press, pull, tear, bite); a still plate gets a push {1.0, 1.10}.
3. **Dialogue on a wide:** remove hesitations with jump cuts at every pause over 0.35 s, keeping the same `zoom` (±0.05) when two or more people are in frame; a jump every 0.5-2 s is normal. Speaker changes are hard cuts 0.0-0.1 s before the new speaker's first word, with no split edit.
4. **Emphasis:** a static `zoom` of about 1.12 (1.07-1.17) on the segment that states the stakes, the prize, a number or the winning answer, back to 1.0 on the next segment 0.3-1.4 s later; a comic snap of 1.5-2.2 for 0.27-0.7 s; 1.33 for a hook reveal.
5. **Reactions** (`beat: reaction`): 0.5-1.1 s each, cut 0.0-0.55 s after the line; 2-4 in a row after a win.
6. **Tasting:** the bite and 0.55-0.9 s of silent chewing before the verdict as a `reaction` segment (so the trim pass keeps it); the verdict line whole (2.3-3.4 s).
7. **Sketches:** one line or try per shot, cut in silence 0.1-0.6 s after the line (up to 1.8 s for a deadpan beat); the payoff in the next shot about 0.4 s after the set-up line; the last image may hold 6-14 s with logo and address 2.5-4 s into it; a button card (white on black, 0.4-0.6 s) 0.0-0.05 s after the line.
8. **Transitions:** `cut` inside a section; one `zoom_in` (0.3 s) or `slide_left` (0.25 s) per section change (3-4 in a 45-70 s reel, one per change of space in a narrated visit); one `dip_to_white` (0.1 s) at most, on the address or a hero product, never on a dialogue cut. No crossfades, holds or slow motion.
9. **Captions** word-timed from the first word: 1 word per screen for fast narration (180+ wpm), 2-3 for dialogue, bold with an outline, uppercase for narration; about 55-60% of the height with two or more people in frame, 70-80% for one person or food. `highlight_words` on the prize, the number, the brand and the right answer. Big titles only on spoken numbers and prizes.
10. **Hook:** frame 0 shows the product in hand or in action, or the person already mid-line; the first word by 0.2 s; a separate hook title only when the voice starts after 0.5 s or there are no word captions. Open on one action (0-1 cuts in the first 3 s); a burst of 5-6 shots of 0.35-0.6 s only for a narrated food montage.
11. **Music:** none under games, reviews, interviews and testimonials; a bed about 4-7 dB under the voice for sketches, cooking or build montages and announcements. Cut to the beat only in a B-roll run with no voice.
12. **Length by format:** games 55-70 s, announcements 45-50 s (a cards-only flyer about 10 s), reviews 25-42 s, sketches 30-47 s, testimonials about 27 s.
13. **Ending:** the address on screen for the last 2-3 s; a brand ending held 1.7-3.4 s after the last word, or a loop that closes on the opening shot with the last sentence cut off. No comment-keyword CTA: a restaurant wants a visit.

## Against the current skills
- **creator-reels** ("alternate wide and close on every cut", "never two takes from the same set-up back to back", "2-6 hard cuts per 45 s", hero words every 2-4 s held 1.5-2.5 s, a bed at medium under talk, a soft effect on every graphic, a comment-keyword CTA, at most 2 whips): each is contradicted here. creator-reels stays right for one person talking to camera; for a restaurant's games, visits and announcements the reels are right (same-size jumps on a group wide, a picture per spoken item, big words only on numbers, no bed under field dialogue, the address as the CTA).
- **retention "hook title from 0.0 s":** only the 3 reels without word captions do it; when the first line is spoken by 0.2 s its captions are the hook title. Keep the rule for late or caption-less openings.
- **retention "6 s with no change feels dead":** a continuous camera move under a running voice, or a payoff the viewer is watching, counts as a change (an 11.8 s orbit in the 4th most played reel).
- **retention "loops work best at 7-15 s":** a loop that ends on the opening shot with the narration's last sentence unfinished works at 47 s.
- **social-short "20-35 s by default", "B-roll 1.5-3 s", "one hero moment like slow motion":** games run 56-70 s, spoken food lists 0.5-0.9 s per shot, and no reel uses slow motion.
- **trailer-and-montage** ("never the same shot twice", "no shot under 0.25 s", "never three of the same kind in a row"): a closing recap flurry of reused 0.13-0.57 s shots and 17 build shots of one kind work here; and a narrated food reel is voice-led, not a montage.
- **The engine's automatic split edits** (0 of 18 use one) and **its 0.75 s pause trim** (this account keeps 0.1-0.3 s, and the trim would cut the silent chew unless it is a `reaction`).

## What the engine can't do yet
- Tracked annotations (a circle following a hand or a walking person); manga speed lines, scribbles, paint-splatter, shatter and ink-blob reveals, the branded stripe wipe; full-frame red or green tint flashes (`adjust` has no hue tint).
- Captions coloured per speaker, captions of non-speech in asterisks ("*rires*"), subtitles that rewrite the speech.
- In-camera transitions (whip pans, a push into blur, a body filling the lens) unless shot; asset stickers (ingredient cut-outs, emoji reactions), a typed-on address, an animated subscribe button.
- Lyric-timed motion cards on a trending song, and the trending audio itself.
